# Proof plan

Status: proposed measurements and experiment design, not completed tests or owner-adopted success thresholds.

## What must become true

| Hypothesis | Evidence to collect | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An owner will buy the system | Signed scoped agreement and collected payment from a suitable venue | Interest without willingness to commit money or resources |
| Guests value the relationship | Paid memberships, benefit use and renewal decisions | Comp-only participation, low use or weak renewal |
| Experience produces return visits | Cohort attendance, repeat visits and reasons for returning | Visits depend entirely on one-time giveaways |
| Coordination is useful | Requests fulfilled, no-show/cancellation rates, staff interviews and measured effort | Staff bypass the system or spend more time repairing it |
| Venue economics work | Membership/purchase contribution less added costs and fee | Gross sales rise while contribution or service quality worsens |
| Regulars can deliver affordably | Actual founder/staff hours and direct cost per venue | The $500 fee buys unbounded manual work |

## Pilot measurement proposal

Before launch agree the pilot window, comparison period, renewal opportunity, target economics and go/no-go thresholds with the owner. Track free versus paid guests separately. Distinguish enrolled, paid, RSVP, confirmed, attended and returned. Keep voluntary reported reasons alongside transaction/attendance records. Attribute outcomes to an identifiable campaign only when evidence supports it; distinguish assisted attribution from causal lift.

Where practical compare similar event nights and account for schedule, capacity, weather, promotions and seasonality. A randomized invitation holdout among eligible opted-in guests can strengthen incrementality evidence if suitable; it is not required or assumed already implemented. A small one-venue pilot primarily tests operation and willingness to pay; it cannot establish broad product-market fit.

Weekly report: active/paid members, occupied lockers/positions, seat requests and fulfilled confirmations, attendance, return visits, renewals/cancellations, approved spend/contribution data, exceptions, digital work delivered and hours/cost. Include denominators and data gaps. Record targets before evaluation so success criteria are not moved afterward.
