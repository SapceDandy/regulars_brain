 Regulars 12 Locker MVP Offer
 Revised October 12 2026. Twelve physical lockers and four bottle positions per locker.

 The MVP has 48 rentable positions. A shared member rents one designated bottle position. A
 whole-locker member reserves all four positions in one locker and is one paying account. Staff controls
 every door and serves the bottles. Four positions are upright spots on the base of a locker, not four
 vertically stacked shelves.

 Use a purchased cube organizer with simple doors, a plain plywood build, or three matching IKEA
 four-cube frames with factory-locking inserts. The working budget uses the factory-locking option because
 its product cost is known and it reduces custom fitting. Your installation labor is contributed to the pilot; it
 is shown separately from cash cost.

   Whole locker monthly                   $125/month. Exclusive use of four positions.

   Shared position monthly                $39/month for one bottle position.

   Alcohol sales                          Bottles purchased separately from the licensed venue. Regulars takes no
                                          alcohol commission.

 These member prices use the earlier whole-locker and shared-position proposals as a starting point. The
 monthly shared price is a new test proposal. The capacity and fee are fixed for this MVP draft; the demand
 assumptions remain editable.

 Who funds the hardware
 Recommended: venue buys the hardware, approximately $663 including planned purchase tax,
 transport, restraints and contingency. Regulars supplies assembly, installation and onboarding labor. Your
 upfront hardware cash is then zero, with an estimated $510 of founder time before ongoing support.

 If Regulars funds the hardware instead, your initial cash is approximately $663 plus any tools you cannot
 borrow. At the modeled $500 fee, recurring contribution is about $333 per venue before hardware
 recovery and company overhead. Hardware ownership, reimbursement and removal rights must be
 written into that arrangement.

 Wall attachment floor bolts and tipping
 For the MVP, let the floor carry the cabinet and bottle weight; attach the cabinet to the wall to
 restrain tipping. A floating wall-hung cabinet is a different design. It must support the entire loaded
 weight through its mounting system, not merely resist a pull at the top.

 Do we have to bolt it to the floor
 There is no universal floor-bolt requirement established by the public sources for every bottle cabinet. A
 floor-supported cabinet can use the manufacturer wall restraint arrangement if the actual wall, furniture
 attachment, load and location are suitable. Follow manufacturer instructions and the local authority
 determination. Floor anchors can be required by a selected design, inadequate wall restraint or a site
 condition; do not add floor holes without checking utilities, slab construction and landlord permission.

 Does it need a minimum weight
 Weight alone is not a safe acceptance rule. Stability depends on depth, center of gravity, door position,
 loading and possible pulling or leaning. No arbitrary minimum pound figure replaces restraint and load
 verification. Keep the heaviest loads low, but do not depend on loose ballast or bottles to prevent tipping.

 Illustration only: an unanchored 200 lb cabinet, with its center of gravity six inches behind the front
 tipping edge, has about 1,200 lb-in of restoring moment. A 40 lb pull at 40 inches creates 1,600 lb-in. It
 can overturn despite being heavy. This simple static example excludes impact and is not an anchor rating
 or installation specification.

 Actual payload and the cheap product limit
 Using a planning weight of three pounds per full bottle, 48 filled positions hold 144 lb of bottles, before
 doors, liners and cabinet weight. Weigh the actual bottles. Shelf load, total cabinet load, wall restraint and
 cabinet attachment are separate checks.

 The selected KALLAX frame lists 29 lb per shelf and 44 lb 1 oz as its wall-cabinet maximum. Sixteen
 bottles at three pounds already weigh 48 lb. That exceeds the listed wall-cabinet figure before
 accessories; this is why the proposed loaded frames stay floor supported. Do not treat the shelf rating as a
 wall-hanging rating. [2]

 Restraint acceptance
 Use the manufacturer specified furniture attachment points and suitable rated connections to verified
 structural wood framing or suitable masonry. Connecting neighboring units is additional to their wall
 restraint. Do not attach restraints only to a thin back panel, unknown hollow furniture section, or
 unverified drywall fixing. The CPSC guides explain locating studs and connecting to sound furniture
 material; they are safety guidance, not commercial bar approval. [5][6]

 Identify wall construction, concealed services and any fire-rated assembly before drilling. Metal studs, questionable
 masonry or a modified cabinet need a competent attachment design. Use manufacturer quantity and fastener
 specifications, document the connections, then check the assembled cabinet under its approved loading and door
 operation. Retighten and inspect after initial service and periodically.

 No new private-club permit is assumed. Sharing four staff-controlled bottle positions does not itself
 change the alcohol sourcing or service rules. The venue remains the seller and controls access, ID checks,
 service refusal and lawful hours. Ask TABC to confirm the complete designated-bottle process, authorized
 users, cancellation and bottle disposition for that venue.

 The selected factory locks do not have unique keys. They are suitable only for the proposed
 staff-controlled arrangement after testing; patrons should not receive keys that can open other lockers.
 [3] If independent customer access is required later, pricing, security and legal review change.

 A legal installation is evidenced by the applicable permit/exemption determination, approved site rights, compliant
 physical location and documented attachment. It is not proved merely by the cabinet being heavy or a consumer product
 being sold online. Confirm contract-required insurance and any final inspections before opening.

 The review allowance is conditional and unquoted; actual permit, structural or professional work may cost
 more. Existing venue inventory can reduce new stock spending. Buying 48 bottles at $30 would cost
 $1,440; do not automatically fill all capacity for the pilot.

 Tool purchase is budgeted at zero if you own or can borrow the required tools with permission. Home
 Depot lists paid tool rentals, so free access is not a general rental policy. Ask the local store which
 purchased-lumber cuts it can provide and any charge. The custom options include a $25 cutting
 allowance; factory assembly needs basic hand tools and the appropriate drill/bit for the wall. Store cuts do
 not perform the entire cabinet build. [13]

 First year cash in the working mixed example
 With all groups active from month one and no churn, operating contribution after replaced drink margin is
 approximately $5,936 over twelve months. Cumulative cash after the modeled venue startup is
 approximately $4,572. Month-one cash change before startup is about $5,103; subsequent months are
 about $76 because annual dues were collected earlier. Hold funds for unearned access, refunds and bottle
 service. These are scenarios, not guaranteed profit.

 Launch and member terms
 Collect refundable founding reservations until physical and operating approval. Start the paid access
 period when the cabinet is accepted and service opens. Monthly cancellations take effect at the paid
 month end; annual members receive clear renewal consent and cancellation terms. Agree any refund and
 lawful stored-bottle disposition with the venue. Do not promise customer removal of a partly used spirits
 bottle or automatic forfeiture/resale after cancellation.

 Staff records member and locker IDs, authorized adults, the specific bottle/SKU, POS receipt, additions and
 remaining contents. Reconcile the physical bottles at close and dues, taxes and costs monthly. Whole and
 shared allocations must never exceed four positions per locker.

 Expansion
 One more matching KALLAX frame plus four locking inserts creates four more lockers and sixteen
 positions. The modeled materials/transport/tax/contingency cost is approximately $222, before your
 additional labor or unusual site work. Another full twelve-locker bank is about $663 on the same
 assumptions. Continue beside the bank or at a second approved location. Do not stack unless
 manufacturer load and connection instructions and the site review support it.
