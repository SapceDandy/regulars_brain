 1. Positioning and your first pitch
 For owners: Regulars helps a venue operate a small locker-membership program, recognize members during
 service and review repeat visits, fulfillment and costs.

 For members: a familiar place with clearly defined privileges: an assigned locker under the venue's rules, a
 simple check-in and access to a limited, bookable seat allocation.

 The first sale is an operating pilot with a clear review date. Your software and marketing skills support it, but
 the owner is buying a manageable program. Start with Regulars Core. The camera/presence-detection concept
 is outside this pilot.

 Your 30-second introduction

 “I'm Devon, and I'm building Regulars here in the Austin area. It helps bars run a small membership around
 lockers, recognition and clearly defined seating perks. I set up the member records, staff process and
 reporting with your team. Then we measure whether people use it, what it costs and whether it's worth
 continuing. Do you already have lockers, or an approved plan to install them?”

 For an existing locker venue

 “You already have the physical program. I'd like to understand where the admin is difficult: knowing who's
 current, tracking bottles, remembering benefits or seeing who comes back. We could test one workflow
 improvement with a small group, then review whether the benefit justifies the fee.”

 For a venue without lockers

 “Before I propose anything, I need to know whether you actually want lockers, where they would go and who
 would fund them. If that isn't a current priority, we should keep this as a later conversation.”

 Gatekeeper / phone opener

 “Hi, who is the best person to speak with about a small membership program for your regular guests? I'm
 looking for the owner or GM who handles guest programs. What's a good time for a 20-minute conversation?”

 Call to schedule before visiting. Ask what time works for that venue; do not assume the lunch-to-dinner gap is
 free. Avoid pitching during a rush, a big game or closing duties. Cluster Round Rock appointments together
 and make separate Georgetown and East Austin trips. Confirm the appointment before spending on travel.

 These drafts are for Devon to review and send. Use actual sender details, a truthful subject and an easy way to
 decline. Do not place placeholders in sent messages.

 2. Discovery, qualification and closing
 20-minute owner call: two minutes on context; ten minutes on the operation; five minutes showing the
 relevant sample; three minutes agreeing a next step.

 Ask in this order, and record the owner's wording:

 1. Who are your regulars, and what do they already buy or pay for?

 2. Which specific weekday/time period would you like to improve? What records show the gap?

 3. Do you have bottle lockers today? How many are usable, occupied, vacant or awaiting repair? If none, is
 installation funded and approved?

 4. What membership or VIP program already exists, what does it charge and where does it create work?

 5. Who would greet/check in members and update inventory on each shift?

 6. Which benefits can you reliably honor, especially during events? What would they cost?

 7. Could we review member dues, matched receipts where possible and staff time monthly? Which fields can
 you actually provide?

 8. Who signs off on spending and the permit-specific bottle rules? If the pilot fits, is $250 setup plus
 $199/month workable?

 Qualify before sending a proposal: an accessible decision maker; usable lockers or funded installation; an
 accountable shift lead; five plausible customers to interview; bounded seat/benefit inventory; feasible records;
 an approved alcohol-service structure; willingness to pay the proposed fee or discuss a narrower paid scope. A
 positive conversation alone is not a qualified opportunity.

 Close the call

 “The next useful step is to inspect [LOCKERS/SPACE] with [MANAGER] and ask five regulars about the actual
 offer. If those checks pass, I'll bring back one written pilot with the cost and responsibilities. Can we set that
 meeting now?”

 Objections and responses

 - “We already do this.” “Great. Which part takes staff time or leaves you without information? If your current
 process works well, there may not be a reason to change it.”

 - “Will this bring more people in?” “That's what the pilot tests. We will separate existing visits from any
 observed change and review costs. I cannot promise an increase before we measure it.”

 - “My staff won't use another app.” “Let's rehearse the simplest lookup and one visit entry on their actual
 device. If it slows service, we fix that before expanding.”

 - “Can you guarantee seats?” “Only for a confirmed booking inside inventory you reserve for the program. The
 pilot will not depend on removing an ordinary guest from an occupied seat.”

 - “Too expensive.” “Let's compare the fee with the work saved and contribution you actually expect. We can
 narrow the pilot; I don't want to sell you a program that loses money without an agreed learning purpose.”

 - “Try it free.” “I can show the sample for free. Setup, training and monthly review are the paid pilot. We
 decide the stop conditions before it starts.”

 - “We don't share POS data.” “We can begin with check-ins and aggregate results you review with us. Spend
 remains unknown where we cannot verify it, so we won't claim sales lift.”

 3. The pilot offer you can take to an owner
 Commercial draft for review. It becomes a venue-specific agreement only after the fields and
 operating terms are approved.

 Term and price: 60 days from agreed launch. $250 setup, due when the roster is configured and training
 delivered at launch. $199 service fee at launch and $199 on day 30. Total proposed 60-day fees: $648 before
 any applicable tax. Continuation after day 60 requires explicit agreement; proposed continuing price is
 $199/month for unchanged scope.

 Included: one venue; up to 20 inspected locker accounts; one agreed check-in/record process; member,
 inventory and seat registers; two 30-minute staff trainings; one monthly owner scorecard/review; up to two
 hours of Regulars support/administration per month. Record usage of that allowance and obtain agreement
 before extra work. The 20-account ceiling is a pilot scope assumption, not measured software capacity.

 Venue provides: functioning/funded lockers, installation and keys; legal alcohol sourcing and service; a
 champion and backup; staff execution; approval of prices, benefits and terms; member billing through its
 existing approved payment process; permitted data and the monthly review. Physical fixtures, construction,
 legal/tax work, advertising purchases, bottle stock and payment processing are outside the Regulars fee.

 Regulars provides: setup, training materials, program administration within scope, measurement and the
 agreed review. The starter workbook is a manual pilot tool. It does not include an authenticated app,
 automated billing, Apple Wallet issuance, POS sync, live multi-device booking or presence detection.

 Launch gates: signed terms and named leads; permitted operating model confirmed; lockers inspected and
 labeled; real seat allocation approved; five paid members or an explicitly documented postponed launch; both
 shifts trained; current roster/pass tests; refund/support process; baseline coverage acknowledged. A deposit
 or expression of interest is not a live membership.

 Review and stop: days 30 and 55. Pause new sales when capacity, stock accuracy or fulfillment fails. Either
 party can stop at the end of the 60-day term. Before signing, agree handling of early termination, undelivered
 setup, unused prepaid services, member refunds and safe closeout of bottle custody. Do not rely on this
 outline as a complete legal contract.

 Pilot decision rules to agree with the owner: target at least five paid members, complete check-ins for at
 least 90% of observed member arrivals, at least 95% of requested benefits fulfilled, no unresolved
 inventory/access incident, and a monthly contribution the owner considers worthwhile. These are proposed
 operating targets, not industry benchmarks. Define the owner's maximum acceptable pilot loss in dollars
 before launch.

 Renewal ask: “Over the pilot, [PAID MEMBERS] joined, [VISITS] were logged, [FULFILLMENT] of requested
 benefits were delivered and your reviewed contribution was [AMOUNT/UNKNOWN]. Here is what remains
 uncertain. Would you continue the same scope for $199/month, change one part of it, or stop?”

 4. Proposed membership and cost test
 Start with one tier unless interviews clearly support both. Existing locker venues should test administration
 and renewal using their current approved terms first; avoid replacing profitable existing dues without counting
 what is lost.

 Single - proposed $99/month: one named adult account and one assigned locker; access through venue
 staff; one active reservation per calendar week for one seat within published two-hour windows; one approved
 food item per week, capped at four per billing month. Food-cost planning allowance: $4 per redemption.
 Membership does not itself include a bottle or an alcohol credit.

 Group - proposed $249/month per group, not per person: one account, up to four named adults and one
 shared locker; up to four seats in one weekly reservation; one shared approved food benefit per week, capped
 at four per billing month, planned cost $8 per redemption. Actual bottle use by named people/guests must
 follow the venue's approved permit model. No unrestricted pooling assumption.

 Shared limits: 21+ program; taxes and any disclosed service fees additional where applicable; staff still
 verifies age and service eligibility. Bottles, ordinary purchases and tips follow the venue's separate prices and
 rules. No unlimited drinks, assumed alcohol discounts, free-drink coupons, unbooked seat guarantee or
 automatic lifetime status. Charges, renewal timing and cancellation instructions must be explicit before
 payment.

 Capacity: one account consumes one locker. Sellable lockers = usable lockers less repair/operating reserve,
 capped at the agreed pilot account limit. For seats, each confirmed group consumes its full reserved seats in
 that slot. Total weekly seat supply cannot prove that a popular individual slot has capacity. Seats are
 confirmed only against the slot ledger.

 Illustrative monthly economics, not a venue forecast: ten singles and two groups produce $1,488 dues.
 At maximum food use, benefits cost $224. Add assumed 3% processing ($44.64), 3% refund reserve ($44.64),
 six venue staff hours at $20 ($120), Regulars fee ($199), locker upkeep ($50) and displaced-seat contribution
 allowance ($150). Illustrative contribution is $655.72 before tax, setup/installation and any additional
 displaced existing dues or food margin. No extra alcohol spending is assumed.

 At five singles and no groups with the same fixed cost assumptions, contribution is -$133.70/month. That is
 why five paid members is a minimum learning sample. Under the single-tier assumptions, seven new singles
 cover recurring modeled costs; ten cover those costs plus the $250 setup fee in month one. An existing
 program must also deduct displaced current dues. Replace every assumed cost with the owner's actual inputs.

 Regulars economics: $199/month less two founder hours at an imputed $50/hour and $25/location
 tool/administration allowance leaves $74/month before selling, travel, tax and overhead. $250 setup
 less six hours at $50 is -$50 before other setup costs. Three venues at this price produce $597 recurring
 gross revenue, not personal take-home pay. The price is for a bounded learning pilot; reprice or simplify if
 actual service time makes delivery unattractive.

 5. Booking, bottle handling and member terms
 Reservation draft: booking opens seven days ahead. The manager publishes eligible dates and exact seats;
 major games, ticketed events and holidays can be excluded before sale. One active booking per account per
 week. Each booking is two hours. The venue confirms through its designated channel; a request is not a
 confirmation. Cancel at least four hours before the slot where possible. Seats release after a 15-minute grace
 period unless the manager approves a delay. Two unexplained no-shows in 30 days trigger a conversation and
 a proposed seven-day booking pause, if that rule was agreed upfront. No extra no-show fee in the pilot. The
 manager handles conflicts and remedies; never remove a seated guest based on an undocumented promise.

 Permit review: a paid membership is not a permit category. TABC's private-club materials discuss locker
 systems under particular permit arrangements; this is not blanket permission for an ordinary mixed-beverage
 bar to use the same structure. TABC also treats alcohol available only to paying customers as a sale for
 licensing purposes and restricts alcohol coupons/rebates. Therefore, the draft offer is built around access and
 food benefits, with any alcohol-specific entitlement unresolved until the venue confirms the proper structure.
 This is a permit-specific review item for the owner and qualified Texas adviser/TABC, not a legal determination
 for any listed venue.

 Give the owner/adviser this exact question list:

 1. What is the exact active permit and licensed premises boundary? Does the proposed locker model fit it?

 2. Who buys and legally owns each bottle, from which approved source, and how is custody recorded?

 3. Who may access and consume it, especially under a group account? Can it be transferred or removed on
 cancellation?

 4. Which storage, service, membership or food fees are permitted, and how are taxes calculated and
 recorded?

 5. What wording may be used in member promotions and referral offers? Are any proposed benefits treated as
 alcohol sales or discounts?

 6. What are the written rules for refunds, renewal consent, lost/damaged stock and program shutdown?

 Member acceptance must name the venue/entity, benefits, exclusions, term, total charge, billing/renewal
 method, cancellation channel, refund/remedy rules, guest access, locker custody and support contact. Use a
 separate optional consent for marketing. Have the venue approve any staff gratuity/service-fee policy; this
 draft adds no automatic gratuity or charge for an otherwise zero-spend visit.

 References: TABC marketing FAQ, TABC license FAQ, TABC private-club NB training, pages 4 and 8, Texas
 Comptroller mixed-beverage taxes. Checked September 25, 2026. The older permit training is background;
 confirm current application to the actual venue.

 6. Five guest interviews and the first 15 prospects
 Ask the manager to identify three people in each of five groups: established weekly regulars; people who
 repeatedly buy premium spirits; organizers of a small friend group; off-peak visitors; guests who have asked
 about storing a bottle or securing a seat. These are prospect categories, not verified buyers. The manager
 keeps private guest data until the person agrees to an introduction.

 Guest interview, eight to ten minutes: “I'm helping [VENUE] assess a possible membership. Nothing is for
 sale until the venue approves the terms. Could I ask about your usual visits?”

 Ask: How many times did you visit in the last 30 days? When and with how many people? What inconvenience
 actually happened on the last visit? Have you paid for a locker or bar membership before, and what did you
 use? Which proposed benefit would change your behavior? Then show the actual one-page offer: “At
 $99/month for this single plan, would you join when it starts on [DATE]? What stops you? May the venue follow
 up once with the final details?”

 For a real group organizer, show the $249 group offer with the same specificity. Do not treat this as a
 statistically reliable price experiment; five interviews identify objections. Record concrete commitments
 separately from compliments. If all five say no, change one major benefit or price and recruit five fresh
 prospects. Do not take nonrefundable payments for an unapproved program. After approval, the venue's
 payment records establish real conversion.

 Staff invitation: “You visit us pretty often. We're opening a small membership with [APPROVED BENEFITS].
 Would you like a 90-second tour and the exact terms? There's no need to decide now.”

 90-second tour: show the assigned locker area; explain who handles service; show the pass/lookup; point to
 actual eligible seats and booking rules; show full price/cancellation; ask, “What would you want clarified before
 deciding?” Close with the venue's approved enrollment step.

 Lost-lead questions: “What held you back: price, benefits, timing or something else? Which benefit did you
 expect that was missing? Would you like one update if we change that?” Record their answer without arguing.

 9. Staff tools, demo and limits
 The workbook is a manual pilot operating kit with editable tables. It uses synthetic rows marked DEMO; no
 real customer records are included. Do not treat the workbook as a secure multi-venue app. Keep the master
 with Devon/owner and issue a separate restricted per-venue staff file/roster before live use. Excel sheet
 protection, if added, is not an access-control system.

 Five-minute demo: open Lookup; enter DEMO-RGL-ATX01-M0001; show the sample account, locker, benefits
 and expiry; scan the printed QR with a text-capable QR reader to obtain the same ID; log a sample visit with
 party size; show Seats with four approved seats and two already confirmed. Type an unknown ID to show
 rejection. The QR does not open a hosted page, authenticate the holder or update billing. Staff checks the
 current roster and identity. A phone camera that does not display text QR payloads needs a QR reader; the
 printed token is the fallback.

 Suggested data flow: the venue confirms payment and terms, then the manager creates the account and
 assigns the locker. Each person authorized for a group is named. Staff checks identity/current term at arrival,
 confirms any reservation, records one check-in, and records benefit fulfillment. Authorized serving staff
 manage bottles under the venue procedure. The manager audits weekly. Devon prepares the monthly report
 from permitted records.

 Identifiers: venue ATX01; locker ATX01-L001; member token unique and revocable; bottle ATX01-B0001;
 visit V000001; reservation RS000001; incident I000001. Do not reuse a retired ID. Live member tokens should
 be randomly generated, with no name, phone or other private data in the QR. Keep the token-to-person
 mapping restricted.

 Bottle recording: retain brand, container size, approved ownership/custody basis, source invoice/reference,
 account, locker, opening measured/estimated volume, signed movements and last physical check. Negative
 mL means poured/removed, positive means a documented correction or addition under venue policy.
 Remaining volume is an operational estimate, not proof of legal compliance or exact liquid measurement. Do
 not erase an error: enter a reversing/correcting event with manager approval.

 Seat editing: one responsible booking editor per shift. Assign unique slot IDs and validate each reservation
 before confirming. The workbook flags excess seats but does not prevent two people from confirming
 simultaneously. If the venue needs simultaneous mobile edits, move the approved schema into an
 authenticated shared application with transactional reservation checks before selling that service.

 10. Operating procedures and rehearsal
 Roles: owner approves offer, permit/tax structure, fees, remedies and access. Champion manages training,
 inventory and slot capacity. Backup covers absences. Serving staff checks approved access, serves and
 records movements. Devon maintains the agreed system, reports and support within scope. Members can see
 only their own approved information through the venue's chosen channel.

 Before every shift: champion verifies active roster, current bookings, blocked lockers, keys and open
 incidents; prepares an approved minimal outage roster; confirms who may edit bookings. Staff gets only the
 fields needed for service. No shared master password and no public links to member data. Remove access
 when staff leave. Agree record retention/deletion with the venue and its legal obligations rather than keeping
 personal records indefinitely.

 Two 30-minute trainings: 5 minutes benefits/limits; 10 minutes pass lookup, identity and check-in; 5
 minutes seat and bottle updates; 10 minutes exceptions. Repeat for both shift teams. Each person performs
 the actions relevant to their role. Record attendance, device used and unresolved questions.

 Seven rehearsal scenarios and expected behavior: normal active member gets the right locker; missing
 pass uses identity plus manager-approved roster lookup; expired member is referred for renewal without
 pretending access; duplicate/unknown token is escalated; a four-person group is checked against named
 permissions and capacity; full slot receives alternatives with no overselling; offline arrival uses the approved
 paper record and is reconciled after recovery. Time ten routine lookups. Proposed target: median under 15
 seconds; no wrong-account or capacity error.

 Exception guide:

 - Lost/shared pass: verify identity through approved venue procedure, invalidate old token, issue replacement
 and document it. A screenshot is never independent proof of current access.

 - Expired/unpaid account: do not edit payment status from a guest assertion. Manager verifies the venue's
 payment evidence and offers renewal or ordinary service.

 - Seat conflict: stop further confirmations, check timestamp/record, offer an approved alternative or remedy
 and log cause. Manager owns the decision.

 - Unnamed group guest: check actual entitlement and permit rules. Escalate; do not improvise bottle sharing.

 - Damaged/missing bottle: isolate affected stock, preserve factual evidence, notify manager and member,
