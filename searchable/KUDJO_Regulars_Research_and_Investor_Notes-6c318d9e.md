 KUDJO + Regulars
 Revised positioning, research and investor preparation
 October 11, 2026


 KUDJO: local commerce through a women’s community
 KUDJO connects women creators and their local relationships with beauty, self-care, local
 self-development and home businesses. Appointments and local pre-orders are separate
 purchase flows. Examples include hair and cosmetics services, massage, plates and
 chocolate-covered strawberries. Restaurants are outside the current launch.

 The sisterhood concept guides the audience and the brand. It does not require every seller to be
 owned by a woman. The founder’s stated access to women who can create promotions is an
 initial recruitment advantage to validate. Measure nearby paying customers and seller
 continuation, alongside following growth.

 Regulars: a system that turns visitors into regulars
 The product digitizes an organic relationship: a guest becomes familiar, their preferences and
 visits are remembered, and appropriate reasons to return continue between visits. The venue
 buys guest continuity and profitable return habits. Guest participation can be free.

 Membership describes one commercial mechanism in the pricing plan. It is too narrow as the
 overall product headline. Lockers, seat access and paid tiers are optional tools within the
 system. The proposed first deployment uses lockers to provide a specific reason to return, while
 the software maintains records and runs approved follow-up.

 KUDJO proof priorities
 Get creator and seller terms before publishing. Match one tagged source to one fulfilled paid
 transaction. Separate services from preorders and organic from paid acquisition. Report creator
 costs, production, verification, refunds and seller sales costs. The illustrative $5 creator
 allocation leaves a $16 media ceiling at zero booking contribution, before general overhead.

 Regulars proof priorities
 Define a regular through observable return frequency over a stated period. Compare free guest
 profiles and locker cohorts. Measure additional venue contribution after displaced ordinary
 purchases, automatic trigger coverage, failed actions and staff time. The revised 80%
 automatic-follow-up target is a proposed learning threshold. The 50% 30-day return target is not
 a mature retention benchmark.

 Platform and market findings
 Use the specific opportunity. Avoid a claim that all small sellers are excluded from existing
 platforms.


 TikTok GO and the EIN distinction
 TikTok’s U.S. GO announcement was May 12, 2026, and centers on lodging and experiences
 through booking partners [4]. TikTok Shop’s seller guide says sole proprietors without an EIN
 can register as individual sellers [5]. These are different products and different eligibility
 questions.

 The defensible KUDJO hypothesis is that a women-centered local creator community and the
 appropriate appointment or preorder workflow can serve these sellers better. Individual-seller
 onboarding, category fit, fulfillment and conversion are the matters to validate. An EIN alone is
 not the exclusion argument. Meta also offers Marketplace seller tools, which belong in the
 comparison [6].

 Individual businesses belong in the market work
 Census reports 12.7 million women-owned U.S. nonemployer businesses in 2022, equal to
 42.7% of that population [3]. This is context across many industries, not KUDJO’s TAM.
 Nonemployer data primarily cover self-employed tax-filing businesses and have receipt
 thresholds. They do not count every informal or occasional seller.

 The salon-only employer count and $606 million fee-pool illustration have been removed from
 KUDJO’s headline market model. Build a category-specific Austin seller inventory, including
 individuals, then verify local buyer demand, seller capacity and net fees. Avoid double-counting
 one person offering several services.

 Regulars belongs in the guest retention comparison
 Square offers visit- or purchase-based loyalty and automated engagement. SevenRooms
 provides guest CRM and behavior-triggered marketing [9–10]. Recognition, automation and
 turning visitors into regulars are existing category promises. Regulars needs a deployment
 advantage, reliable venue fulfillment and measured profitable return behavior.

 The initial bar model uses 40,835 employer drinking-place establishments in 2023 [8]. At $249
 monthly, the full-population fee illustration is $122,014,980 annually. It is not a qualified market
 or adoption forecast. Reaching $100 million at that floor would require 33,468 venues. Broader
 venue categories and higher realized fees are later tests.

 System vision and opening pitch
 The pilots test a focused entry point. The larger products connect verified physical events to
 useful action.


 KUDJO opening
 We are building a local commerce network around women creators and the communities they
 already reach. A recommendation leads to a beauty appointment, a self-care service or a local
 preorder. KUDJO connects that recommendation to a completed paid purchase. We start in
 Austin, prove seller and creator economics, and use permitted outcome history to make the
 local network more useful as it grows.

 Regulars opening
 Regulars turns visitors into regulars. It digitizes the familiarity a venue naturally builds with
 someone who keeps coming back. Guest history remains available across shifts, and approved
 reasons to return continue between visits. Lockers and seats are first deployment tools. The
 software’s larger job is to maintain the relationship with reliable routine automation, measured
 through profitable return visits and less manual administration.
