DEVON DUDLEY / 90-DAY WORKING PLAN


Three ideas.
One clear operating map.
KUDJO
Within 90 days, establish 10 active, fee-generating merchants in one local market.


REGULARS
Within 90 days, operate paid Regulars pilots at three venues with functioning locker programs.


VENTURE STUDIO
By day 90, generate 30 qualified business-buyer leads and five paid brand activations from social
within a rolling 30-day window.


How to use these charts
These are proposed 90-day working targets, not forecasts or claims of current traction. Day 1 is the first day
you execute this plan. Reset the numerical targets after two weeks of real sales and delivery evidence.

Each chart contains one central outcome, eight pillars, and 64 controllable actions. The eight pillar names
repeat in their surrounding blocks: 1 goal + 16 pillar cells + 64 action cells = 81 cells.

An action cadence applies only while that work is active. This is a menu of work, not 192 simultaneous
obligations. Choose at most three setup tasks and three recurring habits across all charts each week.

Shared work counts once. A studio video that helps recruit KUDJO merchants belongs in both systems
conceptually, but it is one content task and one time entry. Track social influence separately from proven
incremental impact.

Turn each box into work
Cadences apply only when the action is active. Use the IDs to find the matching grid box.

K1 Market focus
One buyer segment with a specific, measurable reason to buy.

K1.1 Choose one city + niche / Setup
Pick one city and one merchant category; write who qualifies and who does not.
K1.2 Interview 5 owners / Weekly while validating
Ask five suitable owners about recent acquisition spending, results, and problems.
K1.3 Record real pain / After each interview
Save the owner's exact problem, current workaround, urgency, and decision process.
K1.4 Map quiet periods / Per merchant
Ask which days or hours need customers and what capacity the business can serve.
K1.5 Check sale economics / Before a proposal
Review average spend, variable costs, and the merchant's acquisition-cost ceiling.
K1.6 Write one pilot promise / Per offer revision
Describe one result to measure, a time window, and what KUDJO will deliver.
K1.7 Test willingness to pay / Per qualified buyer
Present explicit fees and responsibilities; record the buyer's actual response.
K1.8 Review objections / Weekly
Group objections and change the weakest assumption in the next sales test.


K2 Merchant sales
A repeatable path from qualified owner to an active campaign.

K2.1 List 25 target owners / Weekly while selling
Research 25 suitable merchants and record the owner, contact route, and fit.
K2.2 Request 10 conversations / Weekly
Send ten tailored introductions or meeting requests to qualified owners.
K2.3 Visit 2 nearby venues / Weekly if accessible
Arrange two nearby owner conversations; count travel inside your work budget.
K2.4 Show one relevant demo / Per interested owner
Demonstrate the referral and purchase journey using clearly labeled sample data.
K2.5 Send a scoped proposal / Per qualified opportunity
Specify campaign length, fees, creator obligations, measurement, and merchant tasks.
K2.6 Follow up on agreed dates / Twice weekly
Follow up on open decisions and stop when the owner declines or opts out.
K2.7 Ask for introductions / After useful results
Ask a satisfied owner for an introduction to one similar business.
K2.8 Review the sales funnel / Weekly
Count outreach, conversations, proposals, launches, and the reasons deals stall.

K5 Customer activation
People reached by creators can understand, try, and use KUDJO.

K7 Economics + trust

K8.2 Keep one campaign board / Each operating day

K8.7 Park unproven features / Weekly
