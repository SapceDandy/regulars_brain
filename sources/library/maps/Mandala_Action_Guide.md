# Devon's Mandala action system

Prepared September 25, 2026. Three completed 9x9 charts, 24 pillars, and 192 controllable actions.

These are proposed 90-day working targets, not forecasts or claims of current traction. Day 1 is the first day you execute this plan. Reset the numerical targets after two weeks of real sales and delivery evidence.

The studio grows your own brands through social media. KUDJO and Regulars are the operating ventures. The studio supplies shared content, distribution, learning, and AI-assisted operations.

Each chart contains one central outcome, eight pillars, and 64 controllable actions. The eight pillar names repeat in their surrounding blocks: 1 goal + 16 pillar cells + 64 action cells = 81 cells.

An action cadence applies only while that work is active. This is a menu of work, not 192 simultaneous obligations. Choose at most three setup tasks and three recurring habits across all charts each week.

Shared work counts once. A studio video that helps recruit KUDJO merchants belongs in both systems conceptually, but it is one content task and one time entry. Track social influence separately from proven incremental impact.

## KUDJO

**Proposed 90-day outcome:** Within 90 days, establish 10 active, fee-generating merchants in one local market.

**Definition:** Active means at least one verified attributed purchase and a collected KUDJO fee in the last 30 days. Seek a second campaign from at least five merchants and positive contribution after variable costs.

**Scope:** Use KUDJO as creator-driven customer acquisition and repeat-business infrastructure. Verify the existing product before promising automated attribution or payouts. Austin is the starting market hypothesis; use another single city if access is stronger.

**Scorecard:** Active paying merchants / verified purchases / repeat campaigns / collected platform fees / contribution after variable costs

**First-week sequence:** Build the merchant list, speak with five owners, and verify one complete referral-to-purchase-to-payout journey before committing to a live campaign.

### K1 - Market focus

**What must become true:** One buyer segment with a specific, measurable reason to buy.

- [ ] **K1.1 - Choose one city + niche** (Setup). Pick one city and one merchant category; write who qualifies and who does not.
- [ ] **K1.2 - Interview 5 owners** (Weekly while validating). Ask five suitable owners about recent acquisition spending, results, and problems.
- [ ] **K1.3 - Record real pain** (After each interview). Save the owner's exact problem, current workaround, urgency, and decision process.
- [ ] **K1.4 - Map quiet periods** (Per merchant). Ask which days or hours need customers and what capacity the business can serve.
- [ ] **K1.5 - Check sale economics** (Before a proposal). Review average spend, variable costs, and the merchant's acquisition-cost ceiling.
- [ ] **K1.6 - Write one pilot promise** (Per offer revision). Describe one result to measure, a time window, and what KUDJO will deliver.
- [ ] **K1.7 - Test willingness to pay** (Per qualified buyer). Present explicit fees and responsibilities; record the buyer's actual response.
- [ ] **K1.8 - Review objections** (Weekly). Group objections and change the weakest assumption in the next sales test.

### K2 - Merchant sales

**What must become true:** A repeatable path from qualified owner to an active campaign.

- [ ] **K2.1 - List 25 target owners** (Weekly while selling). Research 25 suitable merchants and record the owner, contact route, and fit.
- [ ] **K2.2 - Request 10 conversations** (Weekly). Send ten tailored introductions or meeting requests to qualified owners.
- [ ] **K2.3 - Visit 2 nearby venues** (Weekly if accessible). Arrange two nearby owner conversations; count travel inside your work budget.
- [ ] **K2.4 - Show one relevant demo** (Per interested owner). Demonstrate the referral and purchase journey using clearly labeled sample data.
- [ ] **K2.5 - Send a scoped proposal** (Per qualified opportunity). Specify campaign length, fees, creator obligations, measurement, and merchant tasks.
- [ ] **K2.6 - Follow up on agreed dates** (Twice weekly). Follow up on open decisions and stop when the owner declines or opts out.
- [ ] **K2.7 - Ask for introductions** (After useful results). Ask a satisfied owner for an introduction to one similar business.
- [ ] **K2.8 - Review the sales funnel** (Weekly). Count outreach, conversations, proposals, launches, and the reasons deals stall.

### K3 - Creator supply

**What must become true:** Reliable local creators who reach people able to visit the merchant.

- [ ] **K3.1 - List 15 local creators** (Weekly during recruitment). Find 15 creators whose content and audience fit the selected merchant category.
- [ ] **K3.2 - Verify local audience fit** (Before selection). Review audience-location evidence and relevant engagement with the creator.
- [ ] **K3.3 - Send 5 tailored invites** (Weekly). Explain the specific venue, content opportunity, and earning terms to five creators.
- [ ] **K3.4 - Agree clear deliverables** (Per campaign). Record content format, posting dates, tracking link, compensation, and disclosure.
- [ ] **K3.5 - Match creators to venues** (Per campaign). Match audience interests and location to the merchant's offer and capacity.
- [ ] **K3.6 - Walk through first visit** (Per new creator). Explain the visit, post, tracking, and payout steps before the first campaign.
- [ ] **K3.7 - Review tracked results** (Weekly during campaigns). Compare verified purchases and spend with campaign cost; separate clicks from sales.
- [ ] **K3.8 - Resolve payout questions** (Weekly during campaigns). Reconcile approved earnings and answer discrepancies before the agreed payment date.

### K4 - Product + tracking

**What must become true:** A usable, auditable path from a creator post to a verified purchase.

- [ ] **K4.1 - Audit the live product** (Setup). List which signup, tracking, purchase, reporting, and payout features work today.
- [ ] **K4.2 - Test the complete journey** (Before every pilot). Run a referral through signup, verified purchase, fee calculation, and payout review.
- [ ] **K4.3 - Tag every referral link** (Per creator campaign). Give each creator and campaign a unique source link and confirm it records correctly.
- [ ] **K4.4 - Agree purchase evidence** (Before every pilot). Agree how a purchase is verified, using available records and a manual fallback.
- [ ] **K4.5 - Reconcile refunds + repeats** (Weekly). Check duplicate purchases, repeat visits, refunds, and commission adjustments.
- [ ] **K4.6 - Test mobile onboarding** (Per release). Complete merchant, creator, and customer onboarding on a phone and fix blockers.
- [ ] **K4.7 - Watch errors + support** (Each operating day). Check failed tracking events and support requests; prioritize money and access issues.
- [ ] **K4.8 - Fix the top blocker** (Weekly). Ship the smallest change that removes the largest observed conversion or trust problem.

### K5 - Customer activation

**What must become true:** People reached by creators can understand, try, and use KUDJO.

- [ ] **K5.1 - Write a clear visit CTA** (Per campaign). State the business, reason to visit, next action, and any benefit terms plainly.
- [ ] **K5.2 - Show the real experience** (Per campaign). Use accurate venue details and genuine creator footage to explain the visit.
- [ ] **K5.3 - Shorten signup steps** (Weekly during pilots). Observe a new user signing up; remove one unnecessary step or confusing instruction.
- [ ] **K5.4 - Explain tracking + consent** (Per onboarding revision). Explain what is tracked, why, and how the customer can manage their choices.
- [ ] **K5.5 - Invite opted-in visitors** (Per agreed campaign). Send approved reminders only to customers who requested relevant updates.
- [ ] **K5.6 - Check visit friction** (Weekly). Ask recent users what nearly stopped the visit, purchase, or successful attribution.
- [ ] **K5.7 - Test one CTA variation** (Weekly). Compare one message or destination change while keeping other conditions stable.
- [ ] **K5.8 - Review the conversion path** (Weekly). Separate views, clicks, signups, verified first purchases, and repeat purchases.

### K6 - Merchant retention

**What must become true:** Merchants can see enough value to run another campaign.

- [ ] **K6.1 - Record the starting point** (Before a campaign). Capture existing traffic or sales evidence and note gaps in the baseline.
- [ ] **K6.2 - Send a weekly result note** (Weekly during campaigns). Report verified activity, known attribution limits, spend, and the next action.
- [ ] **K6.3 - Review spend with owners** (Monthly). Reconcile tracked results with merchant records and discuss what they mean.
- [ ] **K6.4 - Ask returning-user questions** (Per follow-up sample). Ask whether customers were already regulars and what influenced this visit.
- [ ] **K6.5 - Recommend a next campaign** (After result review). Propose a specific next test based on the merchant's capacity and observed results.
- [ ] **K6.6 - Ask for a paid repeat** (Before campaign end). Request a second paid campaign with explicit scope and fees.
- [ ] **K6.7 - Interview lost merchants** (After a cancellation). Ask why the merchant stopped and record the reason without arguing with it.
- [ ] **K6.8 - Update the playbook** (Monthly). Turn repeated wins and problems into a better onboarding and campaign checklist.

### K7 - Economics + trust

**What must become true:** Merchant value and platform contribution survive real delivery costs.

- [ ] **K7.1 - Document fee rules** (Before selling). Write first-visit, repeat-visit, creator, and customer-benefit rules in plain language.
- [ ] **K7.2 - Model total merchant cost** (Per proposal). Add creator compensation, benefits, platform fees, and any paid distribution.
- [ ] **K7.3 - Separate cash buckets** (Weekly). Track merchant receipts, creator liabilities, customer benefits, and KUDJO revenue separately.
- [ ] **K7.4 - Reconcile earned fees** (Weekly). Match collected KUDJO fees to verified activity and correct discrepancies.
- [ ] **K7.5 - Calculate contribution** (Weekly during pilots). Subtract variable delivery, payment, support, and campaign costs from KUDJO fees.
- [ ] **K7.6 - Cap each pilot's spend** (Before a pilot). Set a spending ceiling and pause any unapproved overage.
- [ ] **K7.7 - Resolve disputes promptly** (Each operating day). Acknowledge disputes, review evidence, and record the final adjustment and reason.
- [ ] **K7.8 - Publish honest proof** (After verified results). Use permissioned case studies and distinguish attributed purchases from proven incremental sales.

### K8 - Operations + focus

**What must become true:** One small team can run campaigns reliably within available time.

- [ ] **K8.1 - Choose one weekly bottleneck** (Weekly). Select the constraint that most prevents the next paying merchant from succeeding.
- [ ] **K8.2 - Keep one campaign board** (Each operating day). Track merchant, creator, due date, status, owner, and the next required action.
- [ ] **K8.3 - Batch repeatable admin** (Twice weekly). Group matching, scheduling, reporting, and reconciliation into bounded work blocks.
- [ ] **K8.4 - Template repeated work** (After the second repeat). Save reusable briefs, onboarding steps, reports, and common support replies.
- [ ] **K8.5 - Review AI-assisted work** (Before use). Check facts, numbers, rights, and tone before using generated material externally.
- [ ] **K8.6 - Log founder delivery time** (Per work block). Record sales, campaign delivery, support, and building time to reveal capacity limits.
- [ ] **K8.7 - Park unproven features** (Weekly). Keep new ideas in a backlog unless they remove an observed launch or retention blocker.
- [ ] **K8.8 - Review 90-day evidence** (Monthly). Compare paying merchants, repeat campaigns, contribution, and effort with the goal.

## REGULARS

**Proposed 90-day outcome:** Within 90 days, operate paid Regulars pilots at three venues with functioning locker programs.

**Definition:** Each venue has existing or funded lockers, a paying Regulars agreement, trained staff, at least five paid members, and recorded visits. Seek renewal decisions from at least two venues whose first pilot term has ended.

**Scope:** Start with Regulars Core: lockers, member and bottle records, staff check-in, seat rules, and owner reporting. The venue runs service and confirms its operating requirements. Presence detection and cameras wait for evidence from Core.

**Scorecard:** Paying venues / paid members by venue / visits by day / fulfilled benefits / venue renewals / Regulars contribution

**First-week sequence:** Identify locker-ready venues, interview three owners, and prepare one specific membership offer and a simple staff check-in demonstration.

### R1 - Venue sales

**What must become true:** Accessible owners commit to a feasible, paid locker-program pilot.

- [ ] **R1.1 - List 10 locker-ready venues** (Setup; refresh weekly). Identify ten venues with existing lockers or a realistic funded installation plan.
- [ ] **R1.2 - Interview 3 owners** (Weekly while validating). Ask three owners about regular guests, quiet periods, space, and service constraints.
- [ ] **R1.3 - Inspect locker readiness** (Before a proposal). Review available space, usable lockers, staff access, and who owns installation work.
- [ ] **R1.4 - Map the revenue baseline** (Before a proposal). Ask for available membership, visit, and spending records; note missing data.
- [ ] **R1.5 - Write one pilot offer** (Per qualified venue). Specify setup work, recurring service, responsibilities, fees, and measurement.
- [ ] **R1.6 - Demo the member journey** (Per interested owner). Show enrollment, member identification, locker lookup, and staff check-in.
- [ ] **R1.7 - Confirm launch ownership** (Before committing). Name the venue sponsor and staff lead; agree access, dates, and operating rules.
- [ ] **R1.8 - Follow up on the decision** (On agreed dates). Follow up on the pilot proposal and record the owner's next decision or objection.

### R2 - Membership value

**What must become true:** Members understand the privileges and the venue can deliver them.

- [ ] **R2.1 - Interview 5 regular guests** (Per pilot venue). Ask five existing regulars what access, recognition, and benefits they would pay for.
- [ ] **R2.2 - Specify exact entitlements** (Before enrollment). Write what the membership includes, excludes, and requires to use each benefit.
- [ ] **R2.3 - Set capacity-based tiers** (Before enrollment). Match individual and group tiers to real locker, seat, and staff capacity.
- [ ] **R2.4 - Calculate benefit cost** (Per tier revision). Estimate the venue's actual cost of each proposed benefit and likely usage.
- [ ] **R2.5 - Write reservation rules** (Before enrollment). Define availability, booking windows, grace periods, guest rules, and release conditions.
- [ ] **R2.6 - Confirm venue-approved terms** (Before enrollment). Have the venue confirm benefit, service, payment, and refund terms for its operation.
- [ ] **R2.7 - Test price with prospects** (Before launch). Present a real price and terms to suitable guests; record objections and commitments.
- [ ] **R2.8 - Refine the weak benefit** (Monthly). Use member feedback and usage records to improve one poorly understood benefit.

### R3 - Lockers + inventory

**What must become true:** Every member entitlement matches a usable locker and accurate record.

- [ ] **R3.1 - Count usable lockers** (Before selling capacity). Inspect and count usable lockers; remove unavailable spaces from sellable inventory.
- [ ] **R3.2 - Assign durable locker IDs** (Setup). Label each locker and connect its ID to the venue's inventory record.
- [ ] **R3.3 - Record assigned members** (Per enrollment). Link the approved member or group to the correct locker and access terms.
- [ ] **R3.4 - Label approved bottle stock** (Per venue procedure). Record each permitted bottle with an ID, owner, and required service information.
- [ ] **R3.5 - Log stock changes** (Per stock movement). Record approved additions, service changes, and removals through the staff workflow.
- [ ] **R3.6 - Reconcile physical inventory** (Weekly during pilots). Compare actual lockers and bottle records; investigate discrepancies with staff.
- [ ] **R3.7 - Document staff access** (Before launch). Agree who may access lockers and how staff record service and exceptions.
- [ ] **R3.8 - Resolve damaged inventory** (When discovered). Record damage or missing items and follow the venue's agreed resolution procedure.

### R4 - Member + staff tools

**What must become true:** Staff can recognize members and fulfill benefits during a real shift.

- [ ] **R4.1 - Build one member record** (Setup). Store membership status, approved benefits, locker, group permissions, and expiry.
- [ ] **R4.2 - Issue a scannable pass** (Per enrollment). Provide a working QR or supported pass linked to the correct member record.
- [ ] **R4.3 - Show a fast staff lookup** (Before launch). Make membership status, locker, and usable benefits easy to find on a phone.
- [ ] **R4.4 - Record visits + group size** (Per member visit). Log the visit, time, and group size through an agreed staff check-in step.
- [ ] **R4.5 - Display seat availability** (Before selling seats). Keep available seat inventory and reservation status visible to the responsible staff.
- [ ] **R4.6 - Keep an outage fallback** (Before launch). Give staff an approved lookup and logging method when the usual tool is unavailable.
- [ ] **R4.7 - Test a busy-shift scenario** (Before launch). Rehearse arrival, missing pass, expired membership, seat conflict, and a group visit.
- [ ] **R4.8 - Fix staff friction first** (Weekly during pilots). Ask staff what slows service and repair the highest-impact problem.

### R5 - Member acquisition

**What must become true:** The right guests understand the program and can enroll without friction.

- [ ] **R5.1 - List likely founding members** (With the venue). Ask staff to identify suitable existing regulars without exporting unnecessary private data.
- [ ] **R5.2 - Film the real experience** (Per launch). Capture approved locker, seating, and service footage with the venue's permission.
- [ ] **R5.3 - Publish a clear member page** (Before enrollment). Show real benefits, price, capacity, venue details, and the enrollment action.
- [ ] **R5.4 - Offer a brief locker tour** (Per interested guest). Show the actual setup and explain benefits and rules before requesting payment.
- [ ] **R5.5 - Train the staff invitation** (Before launch). Practice a short invitation that staff can give to suitable regular guests.
- [ ] **R5.6 - Share one local campaign** (Per launch). Publish approved content through the venue and relevant local creator relationships.
- [ ] **R5.7 - Track each signup source** (Per enrollment). Record whether the member came from staff, social content, a creator, or a referral.
- [ ] **R5.8 - Review enrollment drop-offs** (Weekly during launch). Ask interested nonbuyers what stopped enrollment and fix one repeated issue.

### R6 - Visits + renewal

**What must become true:** Members use their benefits and have reasons to continue paying.

- [ ] **R6.1 - Welcome each paid member** (At enrollment). Explain benefits, check-in, booking, and where to get help in one short welcome.
- [ ] **R6.2 - Choose one quiet-day test** (Weekly with the venue). Select an available low-traffic period and one approved reason for members to visit.
- [ ] **R6.3 - Invite opted-in members** (Per agreed test). Send a relevant visit invitation through the member's chosen communication channel.
- [ ] **R6.4 - Record benefit usage** (Per visit). Log which membership benefits were actually used or could not be fulfilled.
- [ ] **R6.5 - Check absent members** (Weekly). Review recent nonattendance and invite feedback from members who allow contact.
- [ ] **R6.6 - Resolve service failures** (When reported). Investigate an unavailable benefit and agree a repair with the venue and member.
- [ ] **R6.7 - Ask for renewal feedback** (Before term end). Ask what would make the membership worth continuing before requesting renewal.
- [ ] **R6.8 - Review retention by cohort** (Monthly). Compare renewal and usage for members who joined in the same period.

### R7 - Venue results

**What must become true:** The owner can assess repeat visits, costs, and financial value.

- [ ] **R7.1 - Agree a small scorecard** (Before launch). Choose a few shared metrics, definitions, data sources, and review dates.
- [ ] **R7.2 - Compare similar periods** (Monthly). Compare matched days or event types and note seasonality and other promotions.
- [ ] **R7.3 - Request owner sales records** (Monthly). Ask for the agreed aggregate or matched records the owner can actually provide.
- [ ] **R7.4 - Reconcile visits + spend** (Monthly). Compare member check-ins with available records and mark unmatched data as unknown.
- [ ] **R7.5 - Separate revenue streams** (Monthly). Report venue membership receipts separately from Regulars fees and operating costs.
- [ ] **R7.6 - Include service costs** (Monthly). Account for benefits, staff time, refunds, and fulfillment costs in the owner review.
- [ ] **R7.7 - Hold the owner review** (Monthly). Discuss what changed, what remains uncertain, and one improvement to test.
- [ ] **R7.8 - Ask to renew or expand** (At the agreed review). Request continuation based on observed value and capacity; document the decision.

### R8 - Reliable operations

**What must become true:** Each venue has accountable staff and a workable repeatable service.

- [ ] **R8.1 - Name a venue champion** (Before launch). Assign one owner or manager who can resolve questions and approve changes.
- [ ] **R8.2 - Train both shift teams** (Before launch). Rehearse enrollment, check-in, inventory, and exception handling with relevant shifts.
- [ ] **R8.3 - Write the exception guide** (Before launch). Document expired passes, seat conflicts, absent members, damaged stock, and refunds.
- [ ] **R8.4 - Review access permissions** (At setup; monthly). Give staff only the access they need and remove access when roles change.
- [ ] **R8.5 - Log support + incidents** (Per issue). Record the problem, responsible person, response, and final resolution.
- [ ] **R8.6 - Measure service effort** (Weekly during pilots). Record founder and venue staff time spent delivering and supporting the program.
- [ ] **R8.7 - Reconcile Regulars fees** (Monthly). Confirm amounts earned and collected, direct costs, and outstanding venue obligations.
- [ ] **R8.8 - Turn pilots into an SOP** (After each launch). Update one launch checklist from what actually happened before adding another venue.

## VENTURE STUDIO

**Proposed 90-day outcome:** By day 90, generate 30 qualified business-buyer leads and five paid brand activations from social within a rolling 30-day window.

**Definition:** A qualified lead is an owner or decision maker with a relevant need who requests a conversation or pilot. A paid activation is a new fee-generating KUDJO merchant or a paying Regulars venue. Record evidence of the social source and deduplicate each buyer.

**Scope:** The studio builds and grows Devon-owned brands. Its first shared function is social content and distribution for KUDJO and Regulars. Cash comes from owned-brand products and services; audience size is a supporting signal. Broader ventures earn a later test through evidence and available capacity.

**Scorecard:** Qualified social leads by brand / booked conversations / paid brand activations / content hours / attributable acquisition cost

**First-week sequence:** Write each brand promise, choose one buyer-focused content series, record three original videos, and connect every post to a trackable brand-specific next step.

### S1 - Brand clarity

**What must become true:** Every account and post makes its audience and next step clear.

- [ ] **S1.1 - Write each brand promise** (Setup; review monthly). Write who KUDJO and Regulars serve, the problem each solves, and the next action.
- [ ] **S1.2 - Define the studio story** (Setup). Explain the mission of building useful brands and the evidence behind each experiment.
- [ ] **S1.3 - Choose account roles** (Setup). Assign founder/studio content to the shared account and customer education to brand accounts.
- [ ] **S1.4 - Separate buyer messages** (Per campaign). Distinguish merchant, creator, consumer, venue-owner, and member messages.
- [ ] **S1.5 - Write 3 content themes** (Per active brand). Pick three recurring subjects tied to real buyer questions and the brand's value.
- [ ] **S1.6 - Set a simple visual kit** (Setup). Save reusable type, colors, captions, and cover layouts for consistent recognition.
- [ ] **S1.7 - Pin a clear introduction** (Per account). Publish an accurate introduction explaining the brand, who it helps, and where to act.
- [ ] **S1.8 - Audit message consistency** (Monthly). Check profiles, landing pages, demos, and recent posts for conflicting promises.

### S2 - Audience insight

**What must become true:** Content reflects actual local buyer questions and objections.

- [ ] **S2.1 - Interview 3 target buyers** (Weekly during validation). Ask three relevant owners what they need to understand before trying the brand.
- [ ] **S2.2 - Save exact buyer questions** (After each conversation). Add the buyer's own wording to a shared bank of content ideas.
- [ ] **S2.3 - Review local conversations** (Weekly). Read relevant public discussions and save questions connected to the target market.
- [ ] **S2.4 - Study 10 relevant posts** (Weekly). Note topic, opening, audience, and call to action without copying the creator's work.
- [ ] **S2.5 - Tag ideas by buyer** (Per content idea). Label the brand, buyer type, buying stage, and problem each idea addresses.
- [ ] **S2.6 - Check audience location** (Weekly). Review available location evidence and whether responses come from the target market.
- [ ] **S2.7 - Ask one useful question** (Weekly). Publish a focused question that invites buyers to describe a real experience or need.
- [ ] **S2.8 - Rank topics by intent** (Weekly). Prioritize questions that lead toward a relevant conversation, signup, or pilot.

### S3 - Content production

**What must become true:** A small, sustainable process produces useful original posts.

- [ ] **S3.1 - Choose one weekly theme** (Weekly). Pick the buyer question most connected to the current brand bottleneck.
- [ ] **S3.2 - Write 6 opening hooks** (Weekly). Draft six distinct ways to introduce that question without misleading claims.
- [ ] **S3.3 - Script 3 short videos** (Weekly). Write three concise scripts with one idea, real evidence, and one next step each.
- [ ] **S3.4 - Batch-record 3 videos** (Weekly). Record three original videos in one session using demos or approved real-world footage.
- [ ] **S3.5 - Edit with one template** (Per video). Use a consistent layout and remove material that does not help the viewer understand.
- [ ] **S3.6 - Add readable captions** (Per video). Check captions for accuracy, readability, names, prices, and technical terms.
- [ ] **S3.7 - Check claims + permissions** (Before publication). Verify results, customer permission, footage rights, and anything presented as proof.
- [ ] **S3.8 - Save reusable source files** (Per content batch). Store scripts, source footage, captions, and approved versions under the correct brand.

### S4 - Social distribution

**What must become true:** Useful content reaches relevant people and creates conversations.

- [ ] **S4.1 - Pick one primary platform** (Per 30-day test). Choose the platform where you can reach the target buyer with available resources.
- [ ] **S4.2 - Publish 3 original posts** (Weekly total). Publish the three planned pieces across the studio's active content program.
- [ ] **S4.3 - Adapt to one second channel** (Weekly if capacity allows). Rework the same source material for one additional channel without adding a new shoot.
- [ ] **S4.4 - Tag real local context** (Per relevant post). Use accurate location and venue context that helps nearby buyers recognize relevance.
- [ ] **S4.5 - Reply to relevant responses** (Each operating day). Answer genuine questions and route product-specific conversations to the right brand.
- [ ] **S4.6 - Propose 1 collaboration** (Weekly). Invite a suitable venue or creator to make a useful, clearly scoped piece together.
- [ ] **S4.7 - Share in relevant communities** (When permitted and useful). Contribute content only where it answers a real question and fits community rules.
- [ ] **S4.8 - Refresh one useful winner** (Monthly). Update the topic or format that brought qualified conversations with fresh evidence.

### S5 - Lead conversion

**What must become true:** Social interest has a measurable route to a paying brand relationship.

- [ ] **S5.1 - Give every post one CTA** (Per post). Choose one next step for one audience, such as an owner demo or creator application.
- [ ] **S5.2 - Use brand-specific pages** (Setup; revise as needed). Route each audience to the correct KUDJO or Regulars page with a matching promise.
- [ ] **S5.3 - Tag links by source** (Per campaign). Record brand, platform, creator, and campaign in the tracking method available.
- [ ] **S5.4 - Capture lead + permission** (Per inquiry). Save contact details, buyer role, need, source, and permitted follow-up channel.
- [ ] **S5.5 - Respond within 1 workday** (Per qualified inquiry). Reply with a useful answer and a clear next step within available working hours.
- [ ] **S5.6 - Book a discovery conversation** (Per qualified owner). Offer a conversation focused on fit, pain, scope, and a possible paid pilot.
- [ ] **S5.7 - Record paid activations** (Weekly). Confirm the buyer became a paying brand customer and record attribution evidence.
- [ ] **S5.8 - Review the conversion funnel** (Weekly). Compare qualified leads, calls, pilots, payments, effort, and acquisition cost by brand.

### S6 - Shared AI systems

**What must become true:** Automation reduces repeated work while ownership and accuracy stay clear.

- [ ] **S6.1 - Keep one brand knowledge base** (Setup; update weekly). Store current offers, approved claims, buyer notes, and product limits for each brand.
- [ ] **S6.2 - Draft from real interviews** (Per content batch). Use AI to organize permissioned notes into draft scripts tied to actual buyer questions.
- [ ] **S6.3 - Generate hook variations** (Per content batch). Ask for different openings, then select and rewrite those that accurately fit the brand.
- [ ] **S6.4 - Repurpose approved material** (Per content batch). Turn one approved recording into captions, short clips, and a concise follow-up.
- [ ] **S6.5 - Template the weekly report** (Setup). Standardize the few metrics needed to decide which content and brand work to repeat.
- [ ] **S6.6 - Review before external use** (Per output). Check generated content and customer-facing work before publishing or sending it.
- [ ] **S6.7 - Log time + tool cost** (Weekly). Compare saved effort with subscriptions, usage charges, corrections, and maintenance.
- [ ] **S6.8 - Keep a manual fallback** (Before automation goes live). Document how to complete a critical task when a tool, integration, or model fails.

### S7 - Portfolio focus

**What must become true:** Shared marketing advances KUDJO and Regulars without fragmenting attention.

- [ ] **S7.1 - Choose one lead brand** (Weekly). Give priority to the brand with the clearest next paid commitment or retention problem.
- [ ] **S7.2 - Name each brand bottleneck** (Weekly). State the single constraint holding back the next meaningful customer outcome.
- [ ] **S7.3 - Tie content to that bottleneck** (Per content plan). Choose topics that help solve the active brand's sales, activation, or retention problem.
- [ ] **S7.4 - Reuse useful learnings** (Weekly). Share relevant sales lessons and content formats while keeping customer data scoped.
- [ ] **S7.5 - Run one new test at a time** (Per experiment). Define a question, small budget, evidence needed, and review date before starting.
