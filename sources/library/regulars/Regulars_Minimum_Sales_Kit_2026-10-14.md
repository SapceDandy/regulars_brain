# Regulars minimum sales kit

Prepared October 14, 2026. Build deadline: Wednesday, October 14, 2026, at 11:00 p.m. America/Chicago.

This is a working sales and delivery checklist. It includes copy and commercial decisions to complete; it does not represent a published website, activated payment account, signed contract, verified installation quote, or inspection of the current app.

## The outcome to aim for

The venue decision-maker understands the offering, sees a credible demonstration, accepts a defined scope and price, signs the order, completes payment, and books kickoff before the conversation ends.

A same-conversation sale is possible when the person has purchasing authority, the venue fits the offer, and payment and vendor approval are ready. Some venues need partner, landlord, procurement, or compliance approval. When that is the actual obstacle, identify the approver and book the decision meeting.

The initial Regulars proposition remains events, digital growth, and a coordinated guest/member experience, with lockers as the physical launch offering.

Current working targets from the latest offer:

- Venue service: $500 per month, including the agreed events and digital support.
- Physical launch: twelve lockers, with a $2,500 installation target. Final price requires the actual design, supplier costs, site fit, and installation scope.
- No additional percentage of membership or bottle sales is proposed for the pilot.
- Proposed consumer price tests: $49/month Founding Club; $69/month for a named shared-locker position; $99/month whole-locker membership. Locker bundles include the core club membership.
- Alcohol and bottle purchases are separately priced and controlled by the appropriately authorized venue.
- These are planning prices, not confirmed customer demand or demonstrated financial results.

## Freeze the first offer

Suggested commercial structure: a defined 30-day launch pilot at $500 for the venue service, followed by an explicit decision about continuation. Treat this as a recommendation to simplify the first sale, rather than an already adopted agreement.

The latest monthly service scope is:

| Included service | Monthly limit |
| --- | --- |
| Featured campaigns | Two venue-approved occasions, which can build on existing events. |
| Event/guest pages | Up to two dated pages with the agreed RSVP/seat-request, reminder, waitlist, and follow-up workflow. |
| Social assets | Eight reusable posts/story assets from approved venue media; one batched revision round. |
| Google Business Profile support | Initial cleanup and four suitable updates; accurate hours and links. |
| Discovery support | Initial audit and one focused venue-information/page improvement. |
| Review support | A neutral review-request workflow and up to ten response drafts. |
| Regulars tools | The pass/member/seating/locker workflow actually ready for the signed pilot, with a designated staff contact for exceptions. |
| Results reporting | Weekly snapshot where data supports it and one monthly review. |

Specify response hours and actual delivery dates. Restrict the signed product scope to the capabilities you can deliver. If coordination is initially performed by you or staff, disclose that division of work and measure the effort.

The venue supplies a decision-maker, staff lead, real event facts, approved photos, capacity and member rules, on-site service, and timely approvals. Paid ads, SMS/third-party charges, performers, professional photography, extra hardware, custom integrations, and major website work require separate approval and pricing.

Keep two featured campaigns as a combined monthly quota. Do not promise extra campaigns on every channel or unlimited agency work.

## Payment at the end of the meeting

Choose the applicable route before presenting an invoice.

### Route A: the digital launch work can start, but installation is still being qualified

Collect $500 for the first defined service month after signing its scope and start date. This is payment for actual work, not a vague reservation charge. Record it as the first month's fee; do not bill that same month again.

Arrange the locker site/design review separately. The owner must understand that the digital start does not yet provide functioning lockers. It is an optional paid component of the overall rollout, not full locker-MVP readiness.

If the app cannot support the advertised product scope, sell an accurately described founder-delivered service pilot or repair the required workflow before selling that product commitment.

### Route B: installation design, costs, venue fit, and operating prerequisites are confirmed

Use an installation order with an upfront amount that covers the actual external cash requirements. Because the founder has no installation financing budget, an arbitrary 50% deposit may be insufficient.

Illustration only, assuming a confirmed $2,500 installation price and $2,000 of external cost:

| Line item | Timing | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Installation advance | After installation order is signed | $2,000 |
| Installation balance | At documented installation acceptance | $500 |
| First service month | At its separately agreed service start | $500 |

If the service begins at signing, the immediate invoice total in that illustration is $2,500: $2,000 installation advance plus $500 first-month service. Total installation plus the first service month is $3,000. If service starts after installation, do not silently charge it early.

Replace these illustration amounts with the verified supplier, shipping, tax, labor, and mounting costs. Define ownership, what acceptance means, permitted nonrecoverable purchases, change orders, and cancellation/refund treatment before taking installation money. Keep refundable obligations available for refunds.

A successful card payment and usable bank cash are different states. Stripe says a first payout typically completes within 7–14 days, with timing depending on the account and risk. Some bank-debit payments also remain pending before confirmation. Promise procurement and installation dates using the actual account timing. [P2, P3]

Stripe provides no-code customer invoices and payment links. For a negotiated venue order, a customer-specific hosted invoice makes the amount, legal customer, and order reference clear. Keep a QR/link ready, and verify success in your own processor dashboard. Do not collect card details yourself. [P1]

## Website content to prepare

Build one main sales page with these sections. Necessary contact, privacy, and service-term information can sit in short linked pages.

1. Clear owner benefit and contact/demo action.
2. The exact founding pilot scope and fee.
3. A guest and staff demonstration.
4. Locker launch design and its qualification process.
5. Simple illustrative economics.
6. Venue responsibilities and rollout steps.
7. FAQs about staffing, pricing, cancellation, alcohol control, and guest information.
8. Founder identity and real contact information.

Suggested homepage copy, to adapt to verified delivery:

**Regulars helps your venue turn occasions into repeat visits.**

Plan and promote selected events, coordinate guest arrangements, and build a member experience your staff can operate.

**Founding venue service: $500/month.**

The agreed monthly package includes two featured event campaigns, up to two event/guest pages, eight reusable social assets, venue-information support, and results reporting. We configure the guest/member workflow to your approved inventory and operating rules.

**Twelve-locker launch program.**

Add an agreed locker and member program following a site, design, and operating review. Our installation target is $2,500; your written quote confirms the equipment, scope, cost, and timing.

**How we start.**

Choose the first occasion and venue contact. Review the scope and demonstration. Sign the pilot order and pay the agreed initial amount. Complete onboarding and confirm kickoff.

**Speak with Devon about your venue pilot.**

Use your verified contact details and a functioning enquiry or scheduling action. Send an exact invoice once a venue has accepted its scope. Public visitors should not accidentally buy a locker installation before qualification.

Publish only capabilities you can deliver or clearly describe them as scheduled pilot work. Use a real founder identity and a clear contact route. Examples, renderings, and sample numbers must be labeled. Client logos, testimonials, customer counts, and revenue claims require real permission and evidence.

The page and contact form need a plain account of what information is collected, why, and how to contact you about it. Marketing opt-in should be separate from a booking/contact request. Offer guest-data export and access boundaries you can actually honor.

## Three-minute demonstration

Use one coherent venue scenario with sample data:

1. Open a sample occasion page and make a two-person arrangement request.
2. Show the approved capacity and a confirmation or pending-approval state.
3. Show the staff list with names, party size, and the arrangement actually confirmed.
4. Cancel/change a request and show how available inventory and the staff list are updated.
5. Record arrival and show the member/locker assignment only if the workflow exists.
6. Open a sample report linking bookings, attendance, and actual recorded payments where available.

A manually performed step is acceptable for a disclosed pilot. A screen mockup establishes the proposed interface, not operational reliability. Have a short recorded backup and honest labels.

Separately show the proposed physical locker dimensions, quantity, mounting/location, bottle fit, security, and supplier example. Use a rendering if no installation exists yet; label it as the proposed design.

## Owner economics

Use one small model with the venue's own inputs:

- Actual locker/member inventory.
- Proposed monthly dues and expected number of paying members.
- Collection costs, incremental operating costs, refunds, tax treatment, and the $500 venue fee.
- Confirmed installation outlay.
- Any change in contribution from visits, measured without counting existing sales as automatically new.

Illustration: twelve whole-locker memberships at $99 generate $1,188/month of gross dues. After a $500 venue fee, $688 remains before processing, tax, hosting/service, refunds, and other costs. That figure assumes every locker sells and stays paid; it is not profit or a sales forecast.

For an events example, forty genuinely additional $25 visits at a 50% contribution margin generate $500 of contribution before the service fee. That covers the fee arithmetically, while installation and extra event costs still need funding. Validate each input with the owner.

Keep lockers, named shared positions, members, and guaranteed seats separate. Locker membership does not itself create seating capacity.

## Agreement and order requirements

Complete these fields in the agreement before collecting payment. This is a commercial drafting checklist; permit-specific provisions and contractual protections need qualified review before operational use.

| Field | Decision that must appear in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Parties | Correct legal payee/provider, venue legal entity, location, and authorized signers. |
| Purchased scope | Exact first-month services and separately purchased installation; name future capabilities and their delivery conditions accurately. |
| Dates | Signing date, readiness/onboarding conditions, service start/end, first occasion, and installation window if confirmed. |
| Price | Total due now; installation advance/balance; monthly fee; tax treatment; expressly approved extra costs. |
| Renewal | Whether the pilot ends after 30 days or renews; renewal price/date, cancellation deadline and method, and explicit authorization for recurring billing. |
| Failure or cancellation | What happens before kickoff, after service starts, after approved hardware orders, and if installation cannot proceed. Define earned charges and refunds, notice periods, and a resolution contact. |
| Installation acceptance | Written equipment/scope, safe installation requirements, inspection and defect remedy, ownership, warranty, and responsibility for removal/repairs. |
| Revenue and alcohol | Who charges members, holds customer funds, provides refunds, and reports amounts. The permit holder controls the alcohol activities permitted to it. |
| Venue work | Named staff lead; approvals; account roles; media permission; usable capacity; on-site service and event costs. |
| Data and accounts | Venue-account permissions, permitted guest information/use, privacy duties, export, revocation, and return/deletion on exit. |
| Access promises | Exact seat privileges, booking windows, party size, event sessions, grace/no-show rules, and locker/storage rights. |
| Service standard | Support contact/hours, exceptions, measurable deliverables, and no guaranteed attendance, earnings, or search rankings. |
| Vendor prerequisites | Insurance evidence, tax/vendor paperwork, landlord permissions or procurement conditions actually required for that venue and scope. |
| Signatures | Both parties' acceptance with date, and authority to commit the venue. |

Prepare a completed example using a fictional venue so the document has been rehearsed. For a real sale, replace every placeholder and align the website, order, invoice, and onboarding record.

A W-9 contains tax identification information. Have it available privately if requested; never post it on the website. The IRS describes W-9 as a way to give a requester the correct taxpayer identification number. [P4]

## Locker prerequisites

Before representing a particular venue as ready to sell locker benefits, establish:

- Its actual permit type and the proposed bottle ownership, purchasing, storage, and service model.
- Qualified confirmation of that exact operating model; an existing bar license or another venue's locker program does not answer every question.
- Staff-controlled access, member allocations, bottle records, and the handling of departure, nonpayment, breakage, and unused bottles.
- Physical dimensions, load capacity, safe mounting or placement, egress, venue/landlord permission as applicable, and installation responsibility.
- Written quote, funding and cash-availability plan, acceptance process, and realistic delivery dates.

TABC distinguishes mixed-beverage and private-club authorities. Its private-club guidance describes a locker system for privately owned alcohol; that does not by itself approve the planned arrangement at every mixed-beverage venue. Confirm the exact permit and model with TABC or qualified Texas alcohol counsel before offering bottle-storage rights. [P5, P6]

The ordinary paid Regulars membership is not automatically a statutory Texas private club. Do not rely on the brand's use of “club” to establish permitting.

## Onboarding form and delivery plan

Minimum intake fields:

- Venue legal and public name, address, signer, billing contact, and staff lead.
- Contact details and agreed approval/support hours.
- First occasion/date, approved facts/media, venue capacity, and staff expectations.
- Desired member benefits, actual seat rules, locker plan, and relevant permit details.
- Authorized account roles/access; request delegated access rather than passwords.
- Existing guest channels and communication permissions.
- Accepted order reference, payment status, kickoff date, and readiness dependencies.

A proposed first-month sequence to confirm before selling:

| Timing | Delivery |
| --- | --- |
| At acceptance | Signed scope, confirmed payment, intake link, kickoff booked. |
| First two business days after inputs are complete | Kickoff, verify facts/capacity/access, agree the two occasions and staff workflow. |
| First five business days after inputs are complete | First campaign/page and agreed staff view ready for review; correct discovery information. |
| During the agreed service month | Publish approved campaigns, coordinate the agreed arrangements, and log attendance and exceptions. |
| Before pilot end | Review fulfilled scope, contribution inputs, staff effort, and renewal decision. |

Installation has its own validated sequence. Sell only the dates supported by readiness, supplier availability, and payment availability. On-site events remain venue staffed unless an additional staffing service is expressly purchased.

## Same-conversation sales sequence

Suggested structure for a focused 20–30 minute meeting; shorten it when the owner already understands the proposal.

1. Confirm authority and purchasing process: who signs, who pays, and whether anything else must be approved.
2. Ask about a real venue goal: weaker occasions, repeat guests, paid member demand, or guest coordination. Learn existing arrangements.
3. Show the three-minute workflow around that goal.
4. Walk through one pilot offer and the actual cost/work assumptions.
5. Agree scope, responsibilities, start dates, and the applicable installation route.
6. Ask for a decision. If yes, complete and sign the exact order, open its invoice/QR, verify payment, and book kickoff.

Suggested close when the $500 service route fits:

“We've agreed on the first month, the two occasions, and what your staff will handle. The first month's venue service is $500. We can sign that scope, take the first payment, and put kickoff on the calendar now. The locker installation follows its separate confirmed quote and readiness checks.”

Suggested close when the installation is also approved:

“The installation price, equipment, and operating plan are confirmed in this order. The initial amount covers the agreed installation advance and any service starting now. Once we sign and payment is confirmed, we can schedule the next installation step.”

If the owner needs another person's approval, obtain the approver's name, decision criteria, required vendor documents, and a scheduled decision conversation. Do not confuse a verbal yes, signed order, pending debit, successfully paid invoice, or settled bank funds.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Public URL loads on an unfamiliar phone and communicates the correct offer.
- [ ] Every advertised link/action works.
- [ ] Demo and sample figures are honestly labeled.
- [ ] The promised workflow works, or the explicitly sold service can be delivered by the stated people.
- [ ] Pricing, term, scope, cancellation and renewal agree across the page, order, and invoice.
- [ ] Correct legal parties and authorized signer are recorded.
- [ ] Live payment account status is checked; test checkout, status, and receipt flow pass.
- [ ] Initial cash requirements and actual payout timing support any installation commitment.
- [ ] Required venue/vendor approvals are known.
- [ ] First steps, first occasion, staff lead, and kickoff are concrete.
- [ ] Locker claims remain conditional until the actual site, build, and operating model are qualified.
- [ ] A stranger can review the offer, make a decision, sign, pay, and know exactly what follows.
