# Regulars full venue ecosystem and projections

Updated October 13, 2026, Central Texas. This revision restores the broader Regulars vision: prepaid spending and memberships, visit prompting, guest recognition, live game-day participation, in-venue marketing and advertising, affiliate shopping cashback that brings spending back to the venue, and potential lawful gaming partnerships. It replaces the prior locker-only financial presentation for the full concept. It retains $2,500/12 installed as a target and $500/month per venue, with actual install approval and costs pending.

## The idea

Regulars connects what a guest does before, during and after a visit. Before a visit: membership, preferred bottle prepayment, purchased balance, relevant game/event invitations and affiliate-shopping rewards. During the visit: staff recognition, bottle service, controlled booking priority, screens and announcements, public/free participation, sponsor messages and additional food/beer/group purchases. After the visit: rewards and remaining balance/bottles give the guest reasons to return, with consented invitations designed to create additional quiet-time visits.

Free account/card holders also participate. The business does not require every guest to buy a membership. Paid Regulars buys reliable hospitality/access benefits; locker VIP adds storage and service. Purchased points are prepaid venue value, while earned rewards and merchant-funded cashback are separate balances. Top-ups are not income again when redeemed. Regulars should measure the money and visits it adds, not merely move existing spending into an app.

Earlier user discussions described a phased first-six-month approach. Current user steering explicitly asks for all long-term revenue streams. The projections distinguish a limited launch from the full post-MVP ecosystem. No expensive new advertising hardware, travel product, credit card or automatic camera identification is required for launch. Existing approved screens can be used for a small sponsor pilot; full automation follows actual demand and agreements. There are no signed sponsor, affiliate or sportsbook deals identified in this research.

## What is verified versus modeled

Texas Comptroller June–August 2026 monthly alcohol receipts average $45,211 for Finley's at 410 W Main St (permit MB878480), and $154,938 for Third Base at 3107 S Interstate 35 Ste 810 (MB698087). The three records for each permit were queried directly from dataset naix-2893, matching addresses and months. Food sales and unique guest counts are not included. These historical reported amounts do not establish future traffic or demand.

At an assumed $25 alcohol spend/visit and 2.5 monthly visits/customer, the estimated monthly drinking audiences are about 723 and 2,479. These are inferences, not measured identities. First-three-month regular paid-member targets are 5/10/15 at Finley's and 15/30/50 at Third Base, excluding VIP. Lockers fill 4/8/12; the half-whole/half-shared mix gives 10/20/30 paid VIP accounts. The 48-locker mature case needs 120 paid VIP accounts plus 20/60 ordinary members, or 140/180 paid accounts total. That is about 19%/7% of the estimated monthly audience. Finley's 48-locker adoption is particularly ambitious and is not a first-year promise.

Programs such as Four Seasons Bayou & Bottle, Proof, House of Maple, Landry's and House of Blues publicly demonstrate hospitality, identity, access, membership and recognition offers. UPshow publicly demonstrates in-venue screen marketing, scheduling and QR measurement. Those offers verify mechanisms, not our revenue or traffic lift. Rakuten describes advertiser acceptance and commission attribution, and identifies loyalty/subnetwork tracking practices. It does not guarantee our 5% commission assumption or merchant permission. Rates, conversion and shares below are new explicit modeling assumptions, not recovered previously agreed commercial terms.

## Where each number comes from

### 1. Membership and bottle foundation

Membership pricing stays $19/month ordinary; whole VIP $600/year or $60/month; proposed shared VIP $240/year or $25/month. VIP includes standard benefits for its named member, with no second $19 charge. Each new four-locker cohort has one annual whole member, one monthly whole member, four annual shared members and four monthly shared members. Annual prepayments are allocated over twelve months. The resulting earned VIP dues are $290/month per occupied four-locker cohort. Benefit/admin cost is $3.50/member/month, with payment/Billing, refund and conservative unresolved dues-tax reserves. Regular membership contributes $12.786 per member/month; a four-locker VIP cohort contributes $216.5298/month.

Two bottle purchases per occupied locker/month is the user's aim. The illustrative mix is 55% $160 bottles costing $30, 30% $220 bottles costing $55, 15% $350 bottles costing $180. Average price $206.50; cost $60; full future service $16.65; contribution $104.8784 after 6.7% seller beverage tax, 3% processing on the price plus customer tax, $0.30/transaction and 2% waste/refund reserve. These are not actual distributor invoices or verified venue straight-pour prices. Price each SKU against actual equivalent pours; include dues/preparation charges in customer savings. The full 750 ml volume comparison and the financial sixteen-saleable-pour allowance are distinct.

Weighted normal-menu bottle-equivalent contribution is $152.7697. Assume 25% of program bottle sales replace ordinary full-price purchases: deduct $38.1924 lost existing margin per program bottle. Bottle purchase commitment brings money forward, but it does not make every sale incremental or remove future service obligations. Lockers per se are not a protected technical advantage; the service and measurable activation provide the reason to pay Regulars.

Core formula for occupied lockers L, ordinary members R and installed twelve-locker banks B: (L/4 × $216.5298) + (R × $12.786) + (2L × ($104.8784 − $38.1924)) − $541.25 − ($160 × B). The $160/bank includes existing program marketing, member-event allocation, free food reward reserve and upkeep. Additional mature operating reserve of $200 Finley's/$500 Third Base covers shared screen/CRM/affiliate operating effort beyond those baseline costs. These are unquoted allowances; more staffing or software reduces earnings.

### 2. Visits created at times guests otherwise would not attend

Month 1: target 20 net additional Finley's visits or 50 Third Base visits. Month 2: 40/100. Month 3: 60/150. At $25 extra food/beer/non-locker spending and 50% contribution after direct product, tax, service and incentive costs, each net visit contributes $12.50. The quarter therefore contributes $1,500/$3,750. Digital invitations, team/event relevance, remaining purchased balance, bottle service and cashback can trigger these visits.

Mature monthly targets: 200/600 net extra visits × $25 × 50% = $2,500/$7,500. This is roughly 11%/10% against estimated baseline monthly drinking visits. It is a target, not proven marketing lift. Operational targets for linked free-plus-paid guest profiles are 100/300 in Month 1, 200/600 by Month 3, and 400/1,500 in the mature program. A paid member is a subset of those profiles, not a separate extra customer.

To guard against counting visits that were already planned or merely moved from another day, treat only 50% of attributed campaign visits as genuinely additional. Thus the mature gross attributed-visit targets are 400/1,200, with half counted. For causal evidence use a holdout group or randomized invitations where feasible, alongside prior-customer behavior. Do not count a guest arriving because of a notification if that guest was coming anyway.

### 3. Spending while already at the venue

Separate from the new quiet-time visits, target genuinely incremental addons/group spending on existing visits. Month 1: 10 Finley's checks or 25 Third Base checks × $15 additional paid spending × 50% contribution = $75/$187.50. Month 2: 20/50 checks. Month 3: 30/75 checks. Quarter: $450/$1,125 contribution.

Mature: 200/500 existing-visit checks × $15 × 50% = $1,500/$3,750 monthly. These addons must be on baseline visits and exclude both the quiet-time new-visit checks and ticketed experiences below. Existing beer/food purchases that would happen without Regulars do not count. Screens, timely staff prompts, member guests and appropriate group packages are mechanisms to test, not proof of extra purchases.

### 4. Advertising and sponsor packages

Sell a package that can combine authorized venue-screen placements, pass/QR placements and approved game-night/community sponsorship. Do not assume a programmatic TV network pays our direct-sponsor price. A proposed pilot is $250/sponsor/month; mature $300. These are test prices, not market quotations or signed commitments.

Illustrative allocation: 60% venue, 20% Regulars, 20% sales/creative/delivery/payment/tax-exposure reserve. Month 1 none sold. Months 2/3: one/one Finley's sponsor and one/two Third Base sponsors, producing quarter venue contribution $300/$450. Mature: three/six sponsors × $300 × 60% = $540/$1,080 monthly to the venue. Regulars retains $180/$360 before any costs beyond the reserve. Use permitted screen inventory without obscuring games or breaching commercial content rights. Sponsor impressions/QR actions and renewals must be measured. No alcohol-brand funding of consumer rewards is assumed.

### 5. Affiliate shopping creates bar spending value

Example only: a guest makes a $100 qualifying purchase through an approved advertiser's Regulars link. At an assumed 5% finalized commission, $5 arrives. Allocate $3 as fully backed venue spending credit, $1 to the venue, $1 to Regulars. The reward has a funding source; it is not a promise that $100 always earns $3 with every retailer. Returns, disallowed items and attribution failures reduce commissions to zero. Only release rewards after the commission is confirmed/available, unless a separate capped float is funded.

For the pilot, approved qualifying shopping volume is assumed $1,000/$2,000 in Finley's Months 2/3 and $2,500/$5,000 at Third Base. The venue retains 1% of that shopping volume: $30/$75 quarter. Mature volume $10,000/$30,000 monthly produces $500/$1,500 commission; $300/$900 reserved customer cashback; $100/$300 venue share; $100/$300 Regulars share. One operational interpretation is 50/150 qualifying shoppers spending $200/month. This is a hypothesis, not measured shopping volume or a quoted 5% advertiser deal.

Reward-funded redemption sales/margin are not added to the forecast, making that part conservative. The extra-visit and addon rows count new out-of-pocket spending only, excluding credit redemption face value. Buying points/top-ups is likewise not added as profit. A balance cannot be counted at funding and again on redemption.

Cashback requires advertiser approval for a loyalty/incentivized model and proper attribution/settlement. Ordinary Amazon Associates policies explicitly prohibit incentives for using their special links, so it is not the assumed cashback partner. Rakuten supports loyalty/subnetwork tracking, but each advertiser must accept the actual model. Start with properly approved merchants and food/non-alcohol venue-credit terms; wider redemption needs permit/tax review.

### 6. Separate experiences, reservations and game-day participation

Early member gathering and free normal game entry are included in the existing program and generate no extra ticket revenue here. In the mature case, propose separate paid experiences/private-group activity in addition to membership: two Finley's events/month × twenty paid attendees × $20 × 50% contribution = $400; four Third Base events × thirty attendees × $20 × 50% = $1,200. Costs of facilitation, space, fulfillment and benefits are included in the 50% allowance. Pricing, space, audience and costs remain unverified. Do not charge again for a benefit already sold in membership.

Live game prompts, announcements, free predictions and community participation help generate sponsor value, visits and purchases. No paid wagering or alcohol-consumption competition is required. The event row is separately paid activity, not a fee to enter ordinary games that we promised would remain free.

### 7. Gambling is preserved in the vision, with a separate financial branch

Current Texas venue forecast: $0 sports-betting/gambling income in every phase. The Texas State Law Library's July 2026 guide says online sports betting remains illegal; Penal Code Chapter 47 covers gambling offenses. Paid stakes for bar-credit prizes cannot be treated as automatically lawful because the prize is points. Public operation of a fantasy product is not enough to certify it lawful; no gray-market commissions are assumed.

For a later legally permitted jurisdiction and approved licensed-operator affiliate agreement only, an illustrative—not quoted—CPA model is twenty Finley's-equivalent or sixty Third Base-equivalent qualified new customers × $50 confirmed commission × 50% venue share. That would add $500/$1,500 monthly to the venue ($6,000/$18,000 annual), with 30% Regulars and 20% compliance/acquisition/delivery reserve. It is not included in the Texas totals and is not a prediction of Texas legalization. We do not model the bar as bookmaker or take a share of stakes. Actual operator licensing, user eligibility, advertising approval and signed economics are prerequisites.

## What Regulars earns

The $500/month venue fee remains unchanged; no additional cut of bottles or membership dues is added. Sponsor and affiliate shares above are newly proposed commercial assumptions and require agreement, not previously authorized percentages. At maturity, gross monthly Regulars proceeds would be $500 + $180 ads + $100 affiliate = $780 at Finley's; $500 + $360 + $300 = $1,160 at Third Base, before our remaining delivery costs and company overhead. Optional lawful gaming affiliate shares are outside those Texas totals.

The earlier recurring fee contribution of about $315/venue/month uses two support hours at $50, $25 tools, $25 sales allocation, $15 maintenance and payment/Billing costs. A fully automated/sponsor/cashback operation may need more effort; revise costs before promising company profit. The current glass installation budget leaves about $83 before founder installation labor after tax and reserve, so $2,500 remains a target requiring a reviewed, quoted repeatable build. Increasing sales volume does not fix underpriced labor.

## Owner pitch and proof

“Regulars connects your guests to the venue before, during and after their visits. They can prepay for bottles, become members, earn funded bar credit from approved shopping partners and receive invitations that bring them back. While they are here, we connect the experience to useful offers, group activities and sponsors. We run the membership, balance, inventory, screen/campaign and reporting workflows, and measure the additional contribution you keep.”

The 48-locker case is a growth scenario, especially at Finley's. Start with twelve, confirm paying demand, use real supplier/pour prices, and test new visits and addon spend. Expand only with a paid waitlist, reviewed wall/space load, sustained renewals and positive extra contribution. A full 48 bank has about thirty feet of combined two-row frontage before trim, generally requiring multiple banks. The IKEA hanging retrofit/lock layout is not approved by this model.

Reporting must connect consented guest/pass identity, eligible balances, bottle assignment, purchase/refund receipts, event/seat activity, campaign exposure, verified visits and sponsor/affiliate attribution. Put paid/earned/merchant-funded balances in distinct ledgers. Track unearned annual dues, future bottle service and pending cashback. Owner results should distinguish real incremental sales from cash moved earlier, campaign correlation and replaced purchases. Automation/integrations are build milestones, not claims that these features are already operating.

The mature monthly and annual numbers are hypotheses to test. If no incremental visits or addon spend is proven, remove those rows. If no sponsors or accepted cashback partners are signed, remove those rows. Ten sources cannot independently verify a hypothetical future projection; current source facts and all numerical assumptions are separated here.
