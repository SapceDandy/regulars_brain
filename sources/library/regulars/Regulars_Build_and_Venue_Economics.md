# Regulars locker build and venue economics

October 12, 2026. Prices checked for this planning update. USD. The installed price target remains $2,500 total for 12 lockers. The platform fee remains $500 per venue per month. This completes the planning arithmetic; structural design, lock fit, site approval and unquoted costs remain unresolved.

## Decisions and definitions

Twelve physical lockers means three four-compartment modules. Four shared membership positions per locker are account allocations, not four short vertical shelves. Bottles stand full height. Storage capacity is separate from membership count and monthly bottle purchases.

Membership period: $600 is modeled as an annual whole-locker price, consistent with the saved annual model. The user's reference to $500/month at Rowdy is inconsistent with $48,000 upfront and $1,000/locker. We do not treat $48,000 as recurring monthly income. If $600 was intended monthly or for a different term, these projections must be rerun.

The prior capacity model included 60 lockers. We retain 60 as an illustrative later stage, not a physical, legal or demand maximum for a venue.

## Installation and bottle fit

Factory KALLAX is not approved for the proposed full bottle load. IKEA publishes 29 lb per shelf and 44 lb 1 oz total wall-cabinet contents per four-compartment module. Forty full bottles per module at an illustrative 2.5–4 lb each means 100–160 lb of contents, before the cabinet and doors. Three modules at ten bottles per locker mean 300–480 lb of bottles plus cabinet, doors, locks, mounts and reinforcement. A 300 lb total-system promise is incorrect.

A reviewed retrofit must carry each internal shelf into a structural back/frame and then specified wall attachments. Brackets under the bottom alone do not upgrade upper shelves. A custom plywood body with compatible IKEA door components is a development alternative if retrofit fails; dimensions and cost remain unverified. Four brackets per module are a budget quantity, not structural approval. Check actual studs/backing/masonry, unequal left/right loading, joints, shelf deflection, withdrawal, reach and wall condition. Hidden fasteners still penetrate the wall. Never use adhesive suspension.

The CRL no-drill lock is described for glass up to 6 mm and top/bottom mounting on a single door. Its left-edge mounting on this single IKEA door, glass thickness, strike fit, and hinge reversal are not confirmed. Prototype before ordering twelve. Do not drill tempered glass. The IKEA middle glass shelf can be omitted; the factory pull uses a predrilled hole, so removing it leaves a hole.

Fit calculations below assume 12.5 inches clear width × 14 inches clear depth, before trim, liner or hardware. These are unmeasured planning dimensions. Required height is bottle height plus clearance beneath the light. The IKEA insert's published 13-inch external height is not usable internal clearance.

Start with a six-to-nine standard-bottle capacity description pending prototype, while the final allowable count must also satisfy the reviewed weight limit. A 10-bottle design target requires a larger/deeper compartment if the chosen mix does not fit.

Cordless lighting: four Home Depot three-packs, $14.98 each, $59.92 before tax or $64.86 including estimated tax. Includes batteries, adhesive mounts, timer and remotes. Battery runtime and replacement frequency are unverified. Pilot one pack before agreeing to all-night display lighting. Upkeep in the economics below is a provisional allowance, not a battery-life forecast.

Before installation: owner/landlord consent; actual-city permit classification; safe wall/attachment design; fire/egress/accessibility review; lawful lock-work scope; insurance/contract terms; venue permit-specific bottle sourcing, custody and staff service; and tax classification. Austin exempts some cabinet finish work from permits but still requires code compliance. Other cities decide their own scope. Customer-accessible lockers may trigger accessible locker and reach requirements; high mounting is not automatic compliance. The digital ledger never overrides service staff decisions.

## Customer membership menu

| Offering | Upfront annual | Monthly ongoing |
|---|---:|---:|
| Exclusive whole locker | $600 | $60 |
| One of four shared membership positions | $180 | $18 |

These are proposed prices. Shared positions cost more in aggregate because four users require more administration. A shared position does not promise space for ten bottles; the entire physical locker has the measured capacity. Use an initial one-bottle allocation per shared member, with extra capacity subject to staff approval. Whole-locker customers occupy all four membership positions. Never sell whole and shared memberships for the same compartment simultaneously. Bottles are purchased separately through the venue. Monthly is ongoing; a twelve-payment contract is a different product and requires explicit cancellation terms.

For the 12-locker base case: two exclusive annual members, two exclusive monthly members, ten annual shared members and ten monthly shared members. That fills nine physical lockers: four exclusive plus five shared, with three vacant. There are 24 paid accounts and 36 allocated membership positions.

Annual collections: $3,000. Monthly collections: $300. First-month membership cash: $3,300. Earned membership revenue: $550/month, comprising $250/month from annual prepayments plus $300 monthly. At the end of month one, $2,750 of those annual dues remain unearned. Annual collections are not $3,000 of monthly profit. Customer tax on dues is excluded from stated revenue and must be classified before billing.

If all twelve lockers are exclusive annual memberships, upfront cash is $7,200 and monthly earned dues are $600. Twenty-four produce $14,400 and $1,200; forty-eight $28,800 and $2,400; sixty $36,000 and $3,000. Shared-plan economics differ from these exclusive-plan figures.

## Rowdy benchmark and its limits

User-reported figures: $100,000–$150,000 bottle-service revenue in three to four months; $48,000 separate locker upfront receipts; $1,000/locker. None of those sales amounts, actual locker count, time period, bottle mix, average ticket, customer cohort or service margin has been independently verified. The public Rowdy page confirms a locker program, not those financials.

Inferring 48 lockers from $48,000 ÷ $1,000 gives $25,000–$50,000 bottle revenue/month overall, or $521–$1,042 per locker/month, assuming all sales belong to those 48 lockers. That denominator and attribution require verification.

At an illustrative Rowdy $120 average bottle ticket, that implies 4.34–8.68 bottles per locker/month. Applying our $90 price at the same unit frequency gives $391–$781 per fully active locker/month. We use two bottles per occupied locker/month as the base: about 46% of the lower inferred Rowdy frequency. Low is one; high is three. These are judgment-based test assumptions, not a fitted statistical coefficient. A smaller, cheaper program can have lower rather than higher demand. The earlier MVP base used one bottle per whole account and half a bottle per shared account; this two-per-occupied-locker case is a revised scenario, not observed traction.

## Expansion

Add another reviewed four-compartment module beside the existing bank or on a separate approved wall. Each new module has its own structural shelf/frame-to-wall load path. Do not suspend added modules from the original cabinet or stack upward without a revised wall/reach/load review. Twelve/24/48/60 lockers need three/six/twelve/fifteen modules. One side-by-side two-row run would be about 7.53/15.06/30.13/37.66 feet wide before trim, so larger venues will likely use multiple banks. More lockers require inventory controls and demonstrated paying demand, not merely wall space. Twelve-unit lock pricing is $31.89; current 24+ tier $28.05 can lower subsequent purchase costs, but delivery and actual quantities determine savings.

## What Regulars contributes

The locker gives customers a visible connection to the venue. Regulars runs the membership and visit experience around it:

1. Sell and manage whole/shared memberships, payment periods, renewals and capacity without double-booking.
2. Give members a digital pass and staff a quick lookup for active membership, authorized users and locker assignment.
3. Record venue-sourced bottle receipts, size, owner, service activity and estimated remaining servings. Keep an audit trail; estimates do not replace physical inventory.
4. Recognize returning members and communicate useful invitations with consent: quiet nights, tastings, game nights and renewal reminders.
5. Deliver limited, explicitly available member seat/event priority that the venue can honor.
6. Report paid membership, renewals, visits, verified bottle sales, service costs and additional contribution after replacement of existing sales.
7. Train staff, resolve account issues and support the live program within a defined service scope.

The $500/month fee should buy a functioning owner/staff system and execution, not an empty dashboard. No automated sports betting, camera recognition or fully autonomous show control is assumed in this MVP. Later game announcements and member participation features need separate scope and evaluation.

Suggested pitch: “The lockers give regulars a reason to belong here. We install the physical program and run the digital membership experience: payments, passes, bottle records, staff recognition, invitations and owner reporting. We measure whether members return more and produce additional contribution. We start with twelve, prove demand and service, then add capacity.”

At an unchanged $500 platform fee, prior support assumptions yield about $333 recurring Regulars contribution/venue/month: $100 imputed support labor, $25 tools, $25 selling allocation and $16.54 invoice processing. This excludes development, general overhead and income tax; batteries, replacement lights or extra support must be deducted if included in our contract. Ten venues would mean $5,000 monthly fee revenue and roughly $3,335 modeled delivery contribution, not personal take-home. Do not claim larger programs can be supported for the same effort without evidence.
