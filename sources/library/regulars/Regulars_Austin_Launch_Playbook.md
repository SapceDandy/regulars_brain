## 1. Positioning and your first pitch

**For owners:** Regulars helps a venue operate a small locker-membership program, recognize members during service and review repeat visits, fulfillment and costs.

**For members:** a familiar place with clearly defined privileges: an assigned locker under the venue's rules, a simple check-in and access to a limited, bookable seat allocation.

The first sale is an operating pilot with a clear review date. Your software and marketing skills support it, but the owner is buying a manageable program. Start with Regulars Core. The camera/presence-detection concept is outside this pilot.

**Your 30-second introduction**

“I'm Devon, and I'm building Regulars here in the Austin area. It helps bars run a small membership around lockers, recognition and clearly defined seating perks. I set up the member records, staff process and reporting with your team. Then we measure whether people use it, what it costs and whether it's worth continuing. Do you already have lockers, or an approved plan to install them?”

**For an existing locker venue**

“You already have the physical program. I'd like to understand where the admin is difficult: knowing who's current, tracking bottles, remembering benefits or seeing who comes back. We could test one workflow improvement with a small group, then review whether the benefit justifies the fee.”

**For a venue without lockers**

“Before I propose anything, I need to know whether you actually want lockers, where they would go and who would fund them. If that isn't a current priority, we should keep this as a later conversation.”

**Gatekeeper / phone opener**

“Hi, who is the best person to speak with about a small membership program for your regular guests? I'm looking for the owner or GM who handles guest programs. What's a good time for a 20-minute conversation?”

Call to schedule before visiting. Ask what time works for that venue; do not assume the lunch-to-dinner gap is free. Avoid pitching during a rush, a big game or closing duties. ## 2. Discovery, qualification and closing

**20-minute owner call:** two minutes on context; ten minutes on the operation; five minutes showing the relevant sample; three minutes agreeing a next step.

Ask in this order, and record the owner's wording:

1. Who are your regulars, and what do they already buy or pay for?
2. Which specific weekday/time period would you like to improve? What records show the gap?
3. Do you have bottle lockers today? How many are usable, occupied, vacant or awaiting repair? If none, is installation funded and approved?
4. What membership or VIP program already exists, what does it charge and where does it create work?
5. Who would greet/check in members and update inventory on each shift?
6. Which benefits can you reliably honor, especially during events? What would they cost?
7. Could we review member dues, matched receipts where possible and staff time monthly? Which fields can you actually provide?
8. Who signs off on spending and the permit-specific bottle rules? If the pilot fits, is $250 setup plus $199/month workable?

**Qualify before sending a proposal:** an accessible decision maker; usable lockers or funded installation; an accountable shift lead; five plausible customers to interview; bounded seat/benefit inventory; feasible records; an approved alcohol-service structure; willingness to pay the proposed fee or discuss a narrower paid scope. A positive conversation alone is not a qualified opportunity.

**Close the call**

“The next useful step is to inspect [LOCKERS/SPACE] with [MANAGER] and ask five regulars about the actual offer. If those checks pass, I'll bring back one written pilot with the cost and responsibilities. Can we set that meeting now?”

**Objections and responses**

- “We already do this.” “Great. Which part takes staff time or leaves you without information? If your current process works well, there may not be a reason to change it.”
- “Will this bring more people in?” “That's what the pilot tests. We will separate existing visits from any observed change and review costs. I cannot promise an increase before we measure it.”
- “My staff won't use another app.” “Let's rehearse the simplest lookup and one visit entry on their actual device. If it slows service, we fix that before expanding.”
- “Can you guarantee seats?” “Only for a confirmed booking inside inventory you reserve for the program. The pilot will not depend on removing an ordinary guest from an occupied seat.”
- “Too expensive.” “Let's compare the fee with the work saved and contribution you actually expect. We can narrow the pilot; I don't want to sell you a program that loses money without an agreed learning purpose.”
- “Try it free.” “I can show the sample for free. Setup, training and monthly review are the paid pilot. We decide the stop conditions before it starts.”
- “We don't share POS data.” “We can begin with check-ins and aggregate results you review with us. Spend remains unknown where we cannot verify it, so we won't claim sales lift.”

**Follow-up drafts**

Day 2 after the agreed decision date: “Hi [NAME], following up on the Regulars outline. The two open questions are [A] and [B]. Would you prefer to resolve those in a short call, or pause this for now?”

Day 5: “I narrowed the proposed first step to [ONE STEP]. If it fits, could we agree the manager and a date? If it isn't a priority, just let me know and I'll close the loop.”

Day 10: “I'll close out this pilot conversation for now so I don't keep following up. If lockers or member operations become a priority, I'm happy to revisit it. Thanks, Devon.”

Use an owner's requested date instead of the default cadence. Stop immediately on a decline or request for no further contact. Log the next step in Venue CRM.

## 4. Proposed membership and cost test

Start with one tier unless interviews clearly support both. Existing locker venues should test administration and renewal using their current approved terms first; avoid replacing profitable existing dues without counting what is lost.

**Single - proposed $99/month:** one named adult account and one assigned locker; access through venue staff; one active reservation per calendar week for one seat within published two-hour windows; one approved food item per week, capped at four per billing month. Food-cost planning allowance: $4 per redemption. Membership does not itself include a bottle or an alcohol credit.

**Group - proposed $249/month per group, not per person:** one account, up to four named adults and one shared locker; up to four seats in one weekly reservation; one shared approved food benefit per week, capped at four per billing month, planned cost $8 per redemption. Actual bottle use by named people/guests must follow the venue's approved permit model. No unrestricted pooling assumption.

**Shared limits:** 21+ program; taxes and any disclosed service fees additional where applicable; staff still verifies age and service eligibility. Bottles, ordinary purchases and tips follow the venue's separate prices and rules. No unlimited drinks, assumed alcohol discounts, free-drink coupons, unbooked seat guarantee or automatic lifetime status. Charges, renewal timing and cancellation instructions must be explicit before payment.

**Capacity:** one account consumes one locker. Sellable lockers = usable lockers less repair/operating reserve, capped at the agreed pilot account limit. For seats, each confirmed group consumes its full reserved seats in that slot. Total weekly seat supply cannot prove that a popular individual slot has capacity. Seats are confirmed only against the slot ledger.

**Illustrative monthly economics, not a venue forecast:** ten singles and two groups produce $1,488 dues. At maximum food use, benefits cost $224. Add assumed 3% processing ($44.64), 3% refund reserve ($44.64), six venue staff hours at $20 ($120), Regulars fee ($199), locker upkeep ($50) and displaced-seat contribution allowance ($150). Illustrative contribution is **$655.72 before tax, setup/installation and any additional displaced existing dues or food margin**. No extra alcohol spending is assumed.

At five singles and no groups with the same fixed cost assumptions, contribution is **-$133.70/month**. That is why five paid members is a minimum learning sample. Under the single-tier assumptions, seven new singles cover recurring modeled costs; ten cover those costs plus the $250 setup fee in month one. An existing program must also deduct displaced current dues. Replace every assumed cost with the owner's actual inputs.

**Regulars economics:** $199/month less two founder hours at an imputed $50/hour and $25/location tool/administration allowance leaves **$74/month before selling, travel, tax and overhead**. $250 setup less six hours at $50 is **-$50 before other setup costs**. Three venues at this price produce $597 recurring gross revenue, not personal take-home pay. The price is for a bounded learning pilot; reprice or simplify if actual service time makes delivery unattractive.

## 5. Booking, bottle handling and member terms

**Reservation draft:** booking opens seven days ahead. The manager publishes eligible dates and exact seats; major games, ticketed events and holidays can be excluded before sale. One active booking per account per week. Each booking is two hours. The venue confirms through its designated channel; a request is not a confirmation. Cancel at least four hours before the slot where possible. Seats release after a 15-minute grace period unless the manager approves a delay. Two unexplained no-shows in 30 days trigger a conversation and a proposed seven-day booking pause, if that rule was agreed upfront. No extra no-show fee in the pilot. The manager handles conflicts and remedies; never remove a seated guest based on an undocumented promise.

**Permit review:** a paid membership is not a permit category. TABC's private-club materials discuss locker systems under particular permit arrangements; this is not blanket permission for an ordinary mixed-beverage bar to use the same structure. TABC also treats alcohol available only to paying customers as a sale for licensing purposes and restricts alcohol coupons/rebates. Therefore, the draft offer is built around access and food benefits, with any alcohol-specific entitlement unresolved until the venue confirms the proper structure. This is a permit-specific review item for the owner and qualified Texas adviser/TABC, not a legal determination for any listed venue.

Give the owner/adviser this exact question list:

1. What is the exact active permit and licensed premises boundary? Does the proposed locker model fit it?
2. Who buys and legally owns each bottle, from which approved source, and how is custody recorded?
3. Who may access and consume it, especially under a group account? Can it be transferred or removed on cancellation?
4. Which storage, service, membership or food fees are permitted, and how are taxes calculated and recorded?
5. What wording may be used in member promotions and referral offers? Are any proposed benefits treated as alcohol sales or discounts?
6. What are the written rules for refunds, renewal consent, lost/damaged stock and program shutdown?

Member acceptance must name the venue/entity, benefits, exclusions, term, total charge, billing/renewal method, cancellation channel, refund/remedy rules, guest access, locker custody and support contact. Use a separate optional consent for marketing. Have the venue approve any staff gratuity/service-fee policy; this draft adds no automatic gratuity or charge for an otherwise zero-spend visit.

References: [TABC marketing FAQ](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/faqs/alcohol-marketing-practices-faqs/), [TABC license FAQ](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/faqs/tabc-license-permit-faqs/), [TABC private-club NB training, pages 4 and 8](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/static/sites/default/files/2022-02/private-club-wine-malt-beverage-permit-nb-retailers-lc.pdf), [Texas Comptroller mixed-beverage taxes](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/mixed-beverage/). Checked September 25, 2026. The older permit training is background; confirm current application to the actual venue.

## 6. Five guest interviews and the first 15 prospects

Ask the manager to identify three people in each of five groups: established weekly regulars; people who repeatedly buy premium spirits; organizers of a small friend group; off-peak visitors; guests who have asked about storing a bottle or securing a seat. These are prospect categories, not verified buyers. The manager keeps private guest data until the person agrees to an introduction.

**Guest interview, eight to ten minutes:** “I'm helping [VENUE] assess a possible membership. Nothing is for sale until the venue approves the terms. Could I ask about your usual visits?”

Ask: How many times did you visit in the last 30 days? When and with how many people? What inconvenience actually happened on the last visit? Have you paid for a locker or bar membership before, and what did you use? Which proposed benefit would change your behavior? Then show the actual one-page offer: “At $99/month for this single plan, would you join when it starts on [DATE]? What stops you? May the venue follow up once with the final details?”

For a real group organizer, show the $249 group offer with the same specificity. Do not treat this as a statistically reliable price experiment; five interviews identify objections. Record concrete commitments separately from compliments. If all five say no, change one major benefit or price and recruit five fresh prospects. Do not take nonrefundable payments for an unapproved program. After approval, the venue's payment records establish real conversion.

**Staff invitation:** “You visit us pretty often. We're opening a small membership with [APPROVED BENEFITS]. Would you like a 90-second tour and the exact terms? There's no need to decide now.”

**90-second tour:** show the assigned locker area; explain who handles service; show the pass/lookup; point to actual eligible seats and booking rules; show full price/cancellation; ask, “What would you want clarified before deciding?” Close with the venue's approved enrollment step.

**Lost-lead questions:** “What held you back: price, benefits, timing or something else? Which benefit did you expect that was missing? Would you like one update if we change that?” Record their answer without arguing.

## 10. Operating procedures and rehearsal

**Roles:** owner approves offer, permit/tax structure, fees, remedies and access. Champion manages training, inventory and slot capacity. Backup covers absences. Serving staff checks approved access, serves and records movements. Devon maintains the agreed system, reports and support within scope. Members can see only their own approved information through the venue's chosen channel.

**Before every shift:** champion verifies active roster, current bookings, blocked lockers, keys and open incidents; prepares an approved minimal outage roster; confirms who may edit bookings. Staff gets only the fields needed for service. No shared master password and no public links to member data. Remove access when staff leave. Agree record retention/deletion with the venue and its legal obligations rather than keeping personal records indefinitely.

**Two 30-minute trainings:** 5 minutes benefits/limits; 10 minutes pass lookup, identity and check-in; 5 minutes seat and bottle updates; 10 minutes exceptions. Repeat for both shift teams. Each person performs the actions relevant to their role. Record attendance, device used and unresolved questions.

**Seven rehearsal scenarios and expected behavior:** normal active member gets the right locker; missing pass uses identity plus manager-approved roster lookup; expired member is referred for renewal without pretending access; duplicate/unknown token is escalated; a four-person group is checked against named permissions and capacity; full slot receives alternatives with no overselling; offline arrival uses the approved paper record and is reconciled after recovery. Time ten routine lookups. Proposed target: median under 15 seconds; no wrong-account or capacity error.

**Exception guide:**

- Lost/shared pass: verify identity through approved venue procedure, invalidate old token, issue replacement and document it. A screenshot is never independent proof of current access.
- Expired/unpaid account: do not edit payment status from a guest assertion. Manager verifies the venue's payment evidence and offers renewal or ordinary service.
- Seat conflict: stop further confirmations, check timestamp/record, offer an approved alternative or remedy and log cause. Manager owns the decision.
- Unnamed group guest: check actual entitlement and permit rules. Escalate; do not improvise bottle sharing.
- Damaged/missing bottle: isolate affected stock, preserve factual evidence, notify manager and member, record inventory correction and agreed resolution. Staff does not invent liability or refund promises.
- Benefit unavailable: apologize, get manager decision on a preapproved alternative/refund, record requested vs fulfilled status and follow up within one working day.
- Outage: use dated minimal roster and numbered paper visit log; manager verifies uncertainty. Enter each paper record once after recovery and mark reconciled. Keep/destroy the paper under agreed retention rules.
- Marketing opt-out: stop that channel promptly, record suppression and do not add the person back through a different campaign import.

**Support:** service-stopping access, capacity or stock incidents go immediately to the shift manager; Devon handles tool issues within the agreed support window. Set the actual contact and coverage hours before launch. Do not promise 24/7 availability. Lower-priority admin issues receive a next-working-day response target. Owner controls refunds and any operational shutdown.

## 11. Measurement that an owner can trust

**Minimum scorecard:** active paid accounts; unique member visits; quiet-day visits; party size; requested and fulfilled benefits; matched spend coverage; venue program contribution; renewal rate among eligible accounts; Regulars collected fees and service hours. Report sources and limitations alongside the numbers.

**Owner data request:** “For [PERIOD], could you share or review with me member dues collected/refunded, the agreed day's sales before tax/tips, any receipt references that can be matched to visits, and program labor/benefit costs? Four comparable prior weeks would give us a baseline. We can use aggregate totals when individual records aren't available. Please exclude payment-card data and unrelated customer information. Which fields can you reliably provide?”

**Definitions:** one account is one paid membership, including a group account. One visit is one member/group arrival; re-entry is not a second visit unless the venue explicitly defines a new occasion. Party size includes the member. Benefit fulfillment = fulfilled requests / all recorded benefit requests. Spend coverage = visits with a unique matched receipt / recorded visits; unmatched spend is unknown. Renewal = renewed eligible accounts / accounts whose term actually ended. Pending/not-yet-due accounts stay outside that denominator.

**Quiet-day protocol:** choose one low-traffic weekday and matching hours based on records. Gather four comparable baseline periods. Run four weekly test periods with one approved food/access benefit and consistent measurement. Track guest group size, benefit cost, receipt matches, staff effort, weather, special events, holidays and other promotions. Compare per-period averages using equal windows. If practical and consented, invite a randomly selected half of eligible accounts first and the other half later; check fairness and capacity with the venue. A small observational before/after difference remains suggestive, not causal proof.

**Reconciliation:** count each group check once even if multiple named members attend. Match a visit to a receipt only with evidence. Deduct refunds and separate gross receipts from actual cash collections. Keep membership dues separate from food/drink sales, setup fees and Regulars service fees. For a pre-existing membership, subtract displaced old dues when discussing incremental value.

**Contribution:** venue membership dues + any separately supported incremental gross margin, less benefits, processing, refunds, labor, platform fee, upkeep, displaced revenue/margin and allocated setup costs. The starter model assumes no incremental alcohol spend. Regulars contribution uses its own fees minus founder delivery time, tools and actual expenses. Missing owner costs mean contribution is incomplete, not zero cost.

**30-minute review agenda:** 5 minutes member/service facts; 10 minutes receipts, costs and unknowns; 5 minutes guest/staff feedback; 5 minutes one proposed improvement; 5 minutes renewal/stop decision and responsibilities. Ask at day 55 for the next 60-day/monthly agreement so the pilot does not drift into unpaid service.

## 12. First week and the 90-day sequence

**First week, about 8-10 Regulars hours including travel:** 60 minutes reviewing this kit and selecting three prospects; 90 minutes outreach/scheduling; three 20-minute owner calls; one 45-minute inspection plus travel; five 8-10 minute guest interviews at the best qualified venue; 60 minutes finalizing one offer/model; 30 minutes review/buffer. If travel makes this impossible, schedule a video inspection first and one combined on-site visit. Do not expand to ten cold visits before learning from three conversations.

**Days 1-14:** qualify and inspect one venue, complete five interviews, finalize terms and setup. **Days 15-30:** operate the first paid pilot, keep staff records current and publish approved launch content. **Days 31-45:** repair the main service problem and add venue two only if staffing capacity supports it. **By about day 30 for venue two, if two renewals by day 90 are essential:** its 60-day term must start early enough to end inside the horizon. If it starts on day 40, its renewal falls around day 100; do not claim the original renewal goal was met. **Days 46-90:** pursue venue three within capacity and seek renewals from pilots whose terms have actually ended.

Your first three weekly priorities are R1.1 qualification, R1.2 owner conversations and R2.1 guest interviews at the best venue. Activate operational habits when a pilot becomes live. Do not treat all 64 actions as simultaneous weekly homework.

**Readiness signoff:** actual venue/entity and permit; physical locker capacity/funding; approved benefits/prices; approved seat inventory; member terms/payment/refund process; champion/backup; real roster/pass tests; staff access restrictions; both shifts trained; outage/incident path; baseline gaps and cost limits; first review dates. Each unresolved item needs an owner and a due date. Do not mark a draft as an operational completion.

**SOP after each pilot:** record what broke, the observed cause, the fix, who verified it and the version/date. Before adding another venue, ask whether the workflow is repeatable, the next manager can be trained and the current support commitment can still be honored.
