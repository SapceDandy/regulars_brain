**KUDJO and Regulars: where the “whiteout” opportunity actually lies**

Prepared for Devon A. Dudley · October 13, 2026 · America/Chicago

**Research conclusion**

Neither venture currently has a demonstrated “whiteout” that follows merely from its feature list. Local creator referrals, payment-linked attribution, personalized incentives, VIP recognition, memberships, event sales, automated guest communications, and digital entertainment all have substantial precedents.

My strongest candidate for KUDJO is a system that runs joint customer acquisition and return journeys for independent local businesses. My strongest candidate for Regulars is a system that runs repeatable, responsive live experiences inside existing venues. The shared opportunity is to move enough continuing coordination into software that businesses can sustain a service they could not previously operate economically.

Those are hypotheses about a new application and a new level of access. They are not findings that an uncontested category exists. “AI marketing operating system,” “programmable venue,” or “autonomous agent” would remain labels until the product does something specific, reliably, at economics the alternatives cannot match for the intended customer.

**1. What the analogy should mean**

The useful distinction is between selling a technical ingredient and enabling a particular job. A model, payment feed, Wallet pass, creator directory, or generated promotional asset is an ingredient. A customer should be able to describe the useful thing the finished product accomplishes without knowing those ingredients.

There are two credible ways to satisfy the analogy:

| Route | What changes | What would establish it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A different application | Existing ingredients enable a different practical task or experience. | Customers seek and repeatedly use that task; the nearest alternatives do not already deliver the same result under comparable conditions. |
| A different level of access | An existing capability becomes practical for people or businesses that cannot otherwise sustain it. | A material reduction in total operating effort or cost, adoption by prior nonusers, reliable delivery, and a sustainable business model. |

Convenience can matter greatly through the second route. It has to change who can do the job, or how consistently they can do it. A cleaner interface or an extra chatbot alone does not establish that change.

The Christensen Institute describes innovation that reaches prior nonconsumers through accessibility and affordability, and also asks whether the technological enabler fits a sustainable business model. That is a useful test here; it is not a basis for declaring either venture disruptive before observing its market trajectory. [S33]

**2. The product direction used for this assessment**

I treated Devon’s own proposals as the direction and prior assistant suggestions as proposals rather than adopted decisions.

KUDJO’s immediate entry point is creator-posted links to local business pages, particularly for beauty and personal services, with compensation tied to qualifying completed purchases. The discussed commission design includes configurable first-purchase compensation, declining timely residuals, limits, dormancy, and renewed attribution after a qualifying re-engagement. Phone and linked-card identity are intended to reduce continuing consumer effort; Wallet and App Clips are optional access or notification tools.

The longer-term KUDJO direction includes “in-person cookies,” learning from purchase and return behavior, testing relevant offers, coordinating complementary businesses and brands, allocating creator compensation, and eventually supplying useful purchase intelligence. The creator channel is an entry point into that broader system.

AI-generated presenters are a possible production approach, rather than the core customer job. Lower production cost is useful only alongside distribution, trust, completed-purchase economics, and repeat creator or merchant participation. Similarly, a future third-party insights business would need usable information, appropriate rights, and buyers; collecting transaction records does not establish that business.

Regulars’ immediate offering combines a physical locker program with membership, seating and guest coordination, events, and bounded digital growth work. The latest proposal uses a $500 monthly venue fee and a separately quoted installation, with membership prices still treated as tests. The physical installation creates an ongoing relationship; the broader direction connects guest identity, access, venue activity, partners, and eventually operations.

The full Regulars ambition includes a digital environment around the visit: invitations, groups, seating, recognition, member benefits, live announcements, game-linked participation, screen or audio cues, follow-up, and later audience or partner monetization. Hands-free recognition after enrollment is a desired constraint, not a capability assumed proven. Large game-day production remains a later expansion, rather than a required first-three-month launch.

These descriptions are grounded in the current Regulars design, the October 12 KUDJO repository audit, the earlier investor research, and retrieved user discussions. No fresh repository audit or observed venue pilot was performed for this research.

This comparison changes the interpretation of “in-person cookies.” A purchase-linked identity can be useful infrastructure. The cookie metaphor does not mean offline measurement was absent before KUDJO. The product question is what specific decisions become possible and valuable using the information KUDJO can legitimately obtain.

Likewise, recurring commission decay can be a useful contract design. By itself it remains a pricing and attribution rule. It becomes strategically stronger if it supports a demonstrably useful reactivation process, merchant economics, and creator participation that competing arrangements handle poorly.

**4. What survives as the strongest KUDJO hypothesis**

**A system that lets independent local businesses run coordinated customer journeys without each maintaining a continuing partnership and campaign operation.**

The meaningful unit is the whole arrangement: relevant customer, actual merchant availability, permitted budget, suitable creator or partner, delivery of the agreed benefit, completed service, and reconciled compensation. Its value would come from making that arrangement repeatable with low continuing coordination cost.

Consider an illustrative later-stage case in the current beauty-service direction. A nail salon has unfilled appointments. A complementary hair provider has relevant opted-in customers. A creator has suitable local distribution. A cosmetics brand has agreed to fund a defined benefit. The businesses have already approved acceptable participation, limits, and terms.

The candidate product would identify a useful arrangement, make a specific offer at an appropriate time, respect real availability, carry the customer through the qualifying services, account for the benefit and compensation, handle cancellation or refund changes, and report each participant’s outcome. It would then change its next action based on evidence.

The contrast with an ordinary directory or campaign dashboard is practical: the owner should not have to repeatedly find the partner, negotiate the same basic arrangement, construct the campaign, coordinate the booking, chase verification, and reconcile the money. Some of those steps must remain human initially. The whiteout hypothesis concerns whether enough of that work can ultimately be removed to change the economics.

There are substantial precedents even for parts of this larger loop: card-linked networks perform targeting, publishers distribute funded promotions, and marketplaces coordinate merchant inventory. Consequently, the defensible claim is a narrow one about KUDJO’s target participants, complete workflow, interoperability, continuing effort, and results. I did not establish that no company operates the entire combination.

**What the customer gets:** a useful, relevant local purchase journey that is easier to complete.

**What the merchant gets:** an additional way to fill appropriate capacity and acquire or reactivate customers, with understood cost and less continuing coordination.

**What AI contributes:** interpreting business information and context, communicating with participants, preparing suitable arrangements, and coordinating exceptions. Financial eligibility, inventory commitments, payout rules, and budget limits still require reliable authoritative controls. Advanced offer targeting also needs adequate evidence; an LLM alone does not establish causal effectiveness.

**What would make the hypothesis fail:** the company remains a creator-link directory; partners need repeated founder brokerage; funded offers mostly subsidize purchases that would occur anyway; participant incentives conflict; or fully costed merchant/platform contribution is worse than simpler acquisition arrangements.

**5. The difference between knowing a purchase and knowing a person’s intent**

The documented Fidel Select transaction object concerns linked-card purchases at participating locations. It includes payment and merchant/location information and distinguishes authorization, clearing, and refund events. It does not, by itself, provide a record of the products somebody considered, the social videos they watched, or their reasons for choosing a merchant. That limitation follows from the documented transaction event; a richer intent model would need additional valid observations. [S27]

Fidel also documents coverage and timing differences with payment facilitators. For example, some merchant matching occurs only at clearing, so a transaction feed cannot be treated as a universal instant-arrival signal. [S28]

Three different claims must therefore remain separate:

| Claim | Evidence that could support it | What it does not automatically establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A qualifying purchase happened | Matched authoritative payment and fulfillment records | Why the purchase occurred |
| A creator receives credit under the agreed rules | Valid tracked source and the published attribution policy | That the creator caused the entire purchase |
| The intervention produced additional profit | A credible experiment or appropriately qualified causal estimate, with costs | That every tracked purchase was additional |

Research comparing observational advertising estimates with randomized Facebook experiments found that extensive behavioral information still often failed to recover the experimental effects. This is a reason to keep attribution and incrementality distinct, not a transferable estimate of KUDJO’s campaign performance. [S31]

Similarly, Meituan researchers reported an implemented real-time coupon allocation system that combined prediction, budget optimization, and a feedback mechanism. This directly challenges a claim that adaptive incentives alone are new. It does not establish effectiveness for KUDJO’s particular merchants. [S32]

The most useful learning target is therefore specific: which action produces additional contribution for these participants under these conditions? A model that merely identifies likely purchasers can award incentives to people who would have purchased anyway.

The research invalidates my earlier suggestion that deliberately delivering “being a regular” was the invention. Businesses and software platforms already pursue that outcome.

It also invalidates an overly simple distinction between competitors that provide “data” and Regulars that provides “actions.” Several incumbents already connect operational actions with their data. The comparison has to be more precise.

**7. What survives as the strongest Regulars hypothesis**

**A system that makes an existing venue’s night into a repeatable, responsive experience the owner can sustain without continuously producing and coordinating it.**

Here the product is the occasion people experience. Membership, a locker, seating, communications, screens, benefits, and partner arrangements should work together around that occasion.

An illustrative later-stage member visit has several connected commitments. A group chooses an event. The system checks real capacity and the relevant membership rights. It confirms the actual arrangements, prepares the designated host’s briefing, coordinates the agreed communications and display schedule, handles an approved live-event trigger, records benefit fulfillment, and supports the next occasion.

The distinctive aim is to make those interactions respond to the same current situation: which guests and groups have committed, which entitlements apply, what capacity is available, what is happening in the venue, and what actions the operator has allowed. It does not require wagering or a new sports-betting business to have value.

Live cues must also match the experience actually being shown in the venue. A correct external game event is not enough if an announcement arrives before the venue’s broadcast or interferes with service. That is a proposed delivery requirement, not a claim that a timing mechanism has been validated.

This is more specific than making a venue “digital.” It also has to outperform a conventional well-run event using existing membership, reservation, and media tools. If the experience is just a recurring promotion with a television announcement, the whiteout claim would be weak.

The opportunity would be strongest among independent venues that cannot maintain a full event and guest-coordination function, and among patrons who value a recurring experience they can predictably join. The important economic change would be the removal of continuing production and coordination work, while preserving physical service quality.

**What the customer gets:** a coherent occasion worth arranging their plans around and attending again.

**What the venue gets:** an experience it can operate repeatedly, with measurable contribution and manageable staff effort.

**What AI contributes:** interpreting guest requests, adapting communications and approved plans, consolidating the operating information, and coordinating permitted actions. Venue staff still welcome guests, serve them, resolve physical conflicts, and deliver the physical experience.

**What would make the hypothesis fail:** the founder remains the event producer every week; staff do not honor the commitments; members purchase novelty and then churn; added production work outweighs revenue; or the program mainly displaces more profitable ordinary visitors.

A meta-analysis of service relationships found that confidence and social benefits have strong relationships with loyalty. The implication I draw is that Regulars should preserve dependable service and real human connection when automating coordination. It does not show that software, a locker, or an AI-generated greeting creates those benefits. [S35]

**8. Whether “ease” can be the whiteout**

Ease is a viable hypothesis, but its unit of measurement should be continuing operational effort, rather than the number of interfaces.

The relevant comparison is not “Regulars versus doing everything from scratch.” It is Regulars versus the actual combination the owner would otherwise use: existing venue tools, a staff member or promoter, occasional manual work, and possibly no program at all.

For KUDJO, measure the time and cost of obtaining a usable partner arrangement and a profitable qualifying purchase. For Regulars, measure the time and cost of running a satisfactory recurring occasion. Include onboarding, approvals, troubleshooting, owner work, staff work, and founder work. Saving the owner time while transferring all that time to Devon is a service improvement; it has not yet established scalable software economics.

The published Generative AI at Work study found productivity gains in its customer-support setting, with differences across workers. It supports taking AI-assisted coordination seriously. It does not support assuming a venue can eliminate its operating staff or assigning that study’s percentage gain to either venture. [S34]

There is a useful way to state the threshold: a business that previously declined to operate the service should now be able to operate it reliably and profitably. A business already operating it should be able to sustain a demonstrable improvement after all delivery costs.

**9. How the two concepts connect**

The shared technical and economic structure is a set of linked facts and commitments:

| Shared requirement | KUDJO expression | Regulars expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A legitimately identified participant | Customer and tracked creator/partner relationship | Guest, member, group, and venue relationship |
| Current operational facts | Service availability and campaign eligibility | Seat capacity, event schedule, member rights, and observed venue state |
| Defined authority | Approved campaign budgets and partner terms | Approved guest commitments, benefits, media actions, and staff procedures |
| A useful action | Offer, introduction, booking, follow-up, or qualified compensation | Invitation, booking, host instruction, live cue, benefit, or next occasion |
| Delivered result | Completed eligible service and reconciled money | Honored arrangements and recorded participation or fulfillment |
| Economic learning | Merchant and platform contribution from the journey | Venue and platform contribution, repeat attendance, renewal, and operating effort |

The ventures overlap; they are not restricted to separate “acquisition” and “retention” boxes. KUDJO’s proposed return and partner journeys involve retention. Regulars’ events and promotions involve acquisition.

They can share identity tools, communications infrastructure, authoritative records, experiment design, and operating components. They still need separate customer propositions and evidence. A good result in one does not prove the other.

**10. The evidence that would turn the hypothesis into a credible whiteout**

The suggested comparisons below are research designs, not promises of statistical power from a ten-venue pilot. Small pilots can establish feasibility, measured effort, participant willingness, and early economics. Individualized causal targeting requires more evidence.

| Test | Necessary comparison | Main evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KUDJO acquisition entry point | Direct or existing marketplace acquisition and ordinary creator arrangements | Mature paid completions, merchant/platform contribution, creator participation, collection, and refunds |
| KUDJO coordinated partner journey | A single-merchant campaign or simple partner link | Additional useful partner purchases, contribution for each participant, fewer coordination minutes, and repeat participation |
| KUDJO offer selection | No contact, relevant reminder, or simple approved offer rule where feasible | Additional contribution over a defined window, rather than redemption alone |
| Regulars recurring experience | A comparable ordinary event using the venue’s existing tools | Attendance, guest satisfaction, return behavior, contribution, and total production/staff effort |
| Regulars operating reliability | Normal shifts with ordinary staff, not only founder-supported demonstrations | Arrangements honored, exceptions resolved, benefits fulfilled, renewal, and declining founder intervention |
| Accessibility of either service | Target businesses that presently do not sustain the equivalent function | Adoption, continued use, full delivery cost, and customer value sufficient to cover the actual price |

An appropriate decision sequence is:

1. Deliver one narrow workflow reliably.
2. Measure its true continuing cost and the value participants receive.
3. Compare it with the nearest practical alternative.
4. Automate the repeated coordination that the comparison identifies as expensive.
5. Expand only when a complementary workflow benefits from the same permissions, facts, and operating components.

For KUDJO, the October 12 repository audit found that some critical payment, fulfillment, and payout controls remained unproven or failed in the reviewed code paths. That date-bound audit means the immediate prerequisite is a trustworthy purchase record, not a claim that the broader autonomous network already exists.

For Regulars, preserve the locker and bounded events/digital offer as the entry point. One reliable small recurring occasion can reveal the coordination work the future product should take over. The full live sports production vision need not be added to the immediate launch to test that principle.

**11. Important constraints on the larger thesis**

The first constraint is operational authority. Knowing an appointment is available is different from having the right and reliable mechanism to commit it. Knowing a customer is a member is different from the venue honoring the promised seat. Partners and staff must have agreed rules, and the system must work from current facts.

The second constraint is observational coverage. Apple documents passes that become relevant on the device by time or location; that is not, by itself, a documented automatic member-arrival feed to the venue. Apple also documents private Wi-Fi addressing, which limits treating a network identifier as a permanent cross-venue person identifier. Hands-free recognition remains a specific engineering and permissioned-enrollment problem to validate. This research does not propose requiring arrival taps or disabling device privacy features. [S29–S30]

The third constraint is the parties’ economics. A partner campaign can redistribute existing spending rather than add profitable activity. A venue benefit can replace a full-price purchase. A protected seat can displace a valuable walk-in. The system must account for those effects; payment attribution alone will not do so.

The fourth constraint is sufficient useful participation. More merchants or guests improve a system only when they create suitable options, better delivery, or better outcomes. More records are not automatically a moat. The valuable evidence would connect eligibility, action, delivered outcome, costs, and subsequent behavior in circumstances competitors cannot immediately recreate.

The fifth constraint is competing product development. Several incumbents already connect important portions of the same workflow. “They only offer dashboards” is not a defensible assumption. The advantage has to be demonstrated in a specific target segment, deployment pattern, or complete experience.

**12. My independent judgment**

Regulars has the clearer candidate for a different application of AI because the product could change what happens during a night people physically attend. Its largest weakness is the requirement to execute reliably through ordinary venue staff and equipment. A guest-management bundle does not meet that bar by itself.

KUDJO has a clear commercial entry point but more mature analogues to its attribution, targeting, incentive, and referral mechanisms. Its stronger long-term candidate is coordinating independent businesses, customer journeys, and compensation in a repeatable way. That expansion is more substantial than simply adding more purchase data or more personalized messages.

For both, the practical whiteout threshold is the same: the target customer can now sustain a valuable activity that previously required too much continuing coordination, and the product demonstrably carries out the activity with favorable economics.

The best current claim is therefore conditional: KUDJO is working toward operating coordinated local purchase journeys; Regulars is working toward operating responsive recurring venue experiences. The pilots should show which parts of that operation create value. Neither should be called a demonstrated breakthrough on the basis of its ambition.
