## Why these tasks happen at these times

| Scheduling choice | Evidence and practical application | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Hard product/offer work first; 7:15 a.m. most days, 10 a.m. Thursday/Friday | A chronotype experiment found attention outcomes depended on matching task time to individual morning/evening type. Morning is your requested starting hypothesis; late-security sleep shifts the start. | We have not measured your chronotype; exact clock times are unvalidated. HBR's 2026 circadian-rhythm article is management guidance, not a Harvard experiment. |
| One business/task per sprint; batch profiles, templates and records | Leroy's attention-residue research and ready-to-resume experiments support reducing switching and saving a restart plan. | Working on two ventures daily does not require simultaneous demanding tasks. |
| Short recovery between work bouts | A 2022 meta-analysis of 22 independent samples found improvements in vigor and fatigue, while overall performance improvement was not significant. Microsoft's 14-person EEG study concerned video-meeting breaks. | Neither establishes a universally optimal 50/10 or 25/5 timer. Our 50-minute execution, 5-minute handoff, 5-minute break is a planning convention. |
| KUDJO owner outreach late morning/early afternoon; Regulars around 3 p.m. | Tentative business-access windows: try beauty owners between appointments and bar owners before evening service. Ask each owner's preferred time and log replies. | This is operating judgment, not a scholarly claim about optimal sales hours. Move confirmed calls to the owner's availability. |
| Short setup/admin after lunch and evening | Place interruptible, low-complexity work outside protected hard-work sessions; use existing assets and one clear finish line. | We have not established a personal afternoon slump; compare your observed focus/output. |
